**Independent Critique: OTHER GOODS R&D Collection v0.6 Pre-Composition Review**

### 1–3. Methodological Correction Assessment
The shift from “Does Commerce materially change the product?” to “Does Commerce create a tension that would not exist without it?” is a valid and necessary expansion. The earlier test over-privileged elaborate lifecycle mechanics and risked turning the project into a catalog of clever e-commerce edge-cases. Commerce-as-Assertion correctly recognizes that the calm institutional act of pricing, stocking, and offering something unownable can itself generate the required friction.

Risk remains real: if Assertion is used as a soft excuse for products that merely look poetic with a price tag, the store collapses into a poetry book with SKUs. The safeguard is residual power after the first glance. If the product evaporates once the visitor understands the joke or the metaphor, Assertion has failed. If it continues to press on the visitor as an ordinary commercial object, the axis holds.

### 4. Commerce Roles
Commerce-Generated, Commerce-Transformative, and Commerce-as-Assertion are useful observational axes, not a rigid taxonomy. Assertion is distinct: it tests the force of institutional framing itself rather than transformation or creation. Earlier “displayed” products were often weak because they lacked residual pressure. Assertion succeeds only when the calm merchandising of the thing produces ongoing cognitive or affective dissonance. It is therefore a protective axis against over-mechanization, not a retreat.

### 5–9. Unit Types and Scale
Treating the meaningful unit as singular product, relational set, or system-level intervention is a genuine structural advance, not cosmetic. It correctly refuses the assumption that every ontology must live inside one SKU. Product count is rightly decoupled from R&D-unit count. Technical-system products remain useful but must not dominate; temperature diversity is a real constraint, not a quota.

### 13. The World I Helped Break
Works as an OTHER GOODS product. The implication structure (speaker implicated, damage already done, responsibility remains, total control impossible) survives without the source material. It is grand but not primarily cinematic or literary in the store context; the grandness is the point of friction when treated as ordinary merchandise. Commerce-as-Assertion is sufficient and preferable: any elaborate liability or accounting language collapses the emotional register into settlement theater. Title is strong enough to keep.

### 14. “These Days,” Five Years Ago
Distinct from Yesterday. Yesterday exploits the shifting referent of a fixed name. This product exploits the linguistic persistence of present-tense deixis inside a past frame. The core is the strange fact that the past was once “now.” Quotation marks correctly signal that the phrase itself is the object; without them the title becomes softer and more literary. Prefer “Five” over “5” for spoken register. Assertion is sufficient; lifecycle mechanics would over-explain.

No flood of new units required for v0.6 itself.

### 20. Adversarial Critique
Commerce-as-Assertion can become an alibi for weak products that merely look thoughtful with a price. The Singular / Relational / System framework is generative but already risks becoming another taxonomy that the project then has to service. Tolerance Pair is strong until the viewer notices the 0.02 mm threshold; after that it can feel didactic. The World I Helped Break carries residual self-importance and risks reading as literary rather than commercial. “These Days,” Five Years Ago is more clever phrase than deep ontology for some visitors. Human products are being protected partly out of fear of coldness; that protection can itself become a formula. Technical products are sometimes penalized simply for scoring cleanly. The project is currently trying to preserve too many temperatures at once and may be drifting toward a curatorial taxonomy rather than a coherent store. Dependence on external evaluation (including this one) remains a structural vulnerability: the work must ultimately stand without constant methodological justification.

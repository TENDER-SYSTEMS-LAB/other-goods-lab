# OTHER GOODS — Pre-v0.6 Independent Critique

---

## 1–2. Preliminary Note on the R&D Process Itself

Before evaluating the hypotheses: the process is showing a structural symptom. v0.4 was corrected because critics found it sentimental and over-segmented. v0.5 overcorrected toward cold systems, which Section 7 now itself warns against. This is rule-driven correction: each revision fixes the *last* error, not the next one. Four external evaluations produced four different recommendation sets — that is not convergent evidence, it is noise, and building v0.6 as a synthesis of critic feedback risks designing against reviewers (including LLM reviewers) rather than against a visitor. Nothing in the record describes a single observation of an actual human reaching checkout. That is the largest methodological gap in the project.

---

## 3. Hypothesis A — Reduce and Deepen

Directionally correct, but the framing hides a trap. "Does this product survive the full commercial lifecycle?" silently biases selection toward products that *have* lifecycle structure — temporal, systemic, procedural concepts. It will exclude instantaneous products whose strength is the moment, not the arc (`17,400 Hz` is strongest as a synchronous event; at t+6 months it is nothing, and that is fine). The criterion should be: **survive the lifecycle, or justify its absence.** A product may fail at t+6 months on purpose.

Second trap: 12–16 bespoke, deeply engineered products is a gallery, not a store. Real commercial realism comes from *density and mundanity* — a hundred boring SKUs surrounding the strange ones. A store of fourteen novelties, each with a bespoke price-decay function, reads as a designer boutique, which is less "credible commercial infrastructure," not more. The resolution: v0.6 is R&D, not storefront composition. Deepen 12–16, but the depth must produce **reusable mechanics and a default-policy layer** so that scaling to 200 SKUs later costs almost nothing per unit. If every v0.6 product requires its own 17-field sheet, the scaling plan is dead on arrival.

---

## 4. Post-Purchase Rule — Critique

The proposed rule — *"if post-purchase behavior reveals conceptual depth, it must be designed; if it adds only a joke, do not force it"* — is not operational. It requires knowing, at design time, whether depth will be "revealed," and it delegates the depth/joke distinction to taste without a test. As written, it constrains nothing.

Replace it with a mandatory question and a conditional build:

> **Mandatory for all products:** Answer, in one line each, what the customer receives and what the system records at t+0, t+1 day, and t+6 months.
> **Mandatory design only when:** the system's record diverges from what the customer believes they bought. Divergence is the criterion; depth is the outcome.

Should post-purchase behavior be mandatory for every product? **No.** The three examples given (Yesterday, First Canceled Order, Dented in Transit) all share temporal or state-change structure; not all products do. Forcing lifecycle onto a synchronous product produces symmetric bureaucracy — and the joke-risk the rule fears. Note also that some products' *purchase act is the fulfillment* (First Canceled Order); the lifecycle framework should accommodate products that end at t+0.

---

## 5. Price and Inventory Logic

The team is asking the right question under the wrong field name. "Why does this cost this amount?" is downstream of the real question:

> **Unit Definition: what exactly is one unit, and why is it divisible or not?**

This field is missing from every list in the document, including the Section 17 sheet, despite Section 5 raising it. Price logic and inventory logic mostly *follow* from unit definition.

- **Price Logic:** mandatory as a *question*, not as differentiation. "Fixed, market-standard" is a valid answer and the correct answer for most SKUs. Demanding conceptual price logic everywhere re-imports the over-engineering problem and makes pricing itself a formula.
- **Inventory Logic:** same structure, weaker necessity. Most unownables are non-rivalrous; infinite stock is the honest default and does not need a field. Inventory logic becomes mandatory only where scarcity or regeneration is constitutive: `First Canceled Order` should be quantity 1; `Yesterday` should regenerate at midnight — that daily restock is arguably the strongest inventory mechanic the project has found, and it is hiding in a v0.1 product.

Rule of thumb: Commerce-Generated and Commerce-Activated products must differentiate price and inventory. Commerce-Displayed products may have a flat price and infinite stock, *if* they earn their place by some other means.

---

## 6. Register Fidelity — Evaluation

Register Fidelity is stronger than Dryness, and for a precise reason: it explains *why* dry names worked. They worked not because they were dry but because they were idiomatic to a system. Dryness-as-goal produced "bureaucratic cool" as house style; register-as-source produces variety with internal logic.

Three qualifications:

1. **Mass-familiarity constraint.** A register only transmits affect if the visitor knows it. Credit cards, shipping, receipts, warranties: universal. Actuarial tables, maritime liens, estoppel: opaque, and the name dies on the page. Restrict the rule to registers a non-specialist has personally received paperwork from.
2. **Register belongs to paperwork more than titles.** The strongest v0.5 names (`Yesterday`, `First Typo in a New Notebook`) are neither dry nor register-bound — they are plain speech. The rule should govern system language (metadata, policies, confirmations) and permit plain titles.
3. **Yes, it can become a device.** If every product wears a different professional uniform, the reader learns the trick within three pages; register-collecting (aviation! insurance! printing!) becomes exoticism-shopping with the same predictability as dryness.

One counter-move the project has not considered: the driest thing left is **total mundanity**. A product named like a real SKU — `White Mug, 11 oz` — is now the loudest possible inversion in this context, because every OTHER GOODS name currently signals its own strangeness through the register/number formula. One or two fully mundane names would reset the reader's pattern detection.

## 7. Uniform System, Variable Temperatures

This is the strongest idea in the document, and it *is* a better interpretation of Progressive Displacement (assuming that term meant warm→cold movement; the document never defines it — itself a symptom). The artwork is a **temperature differential**: one indifferent machine, objects of radically different heat. Two corollaries must be adopted as law:

- **All variance belongs to the objects. None belongs to the system.** The store must never react to a product's weight — no special banners, no altered microcopy, no winking. The moment the system acknowledges the differential, it collapses.
- This is actually an argument *for* uniform lifecycle processing: the machine's uniformity is what makes object variance legible. Hypothesis B should be reframed accordingly — not "give Commerce meaningful work" (which invites engineered twists per product) but "process everything identically, and let the identical processing be the pressure test."

Also note: Section 15 asks reviewers to score "Residue 1–5" — a metric defined nowhere in the document. When a team scores undefined quantities, taxonomy has replaced judgment. Flag this now.

## 8–9. Moral Residue — The Six Questions

1. **Genuinely new ontology?** Partially. *Consequences that outlive settlement* is a real object-state absent from the current territory map. *Responsibility beyond control* and *finite responsibility* are postures, not states — they fail the basic test: cannot be possessed, listed, delivered, or returned. The Nolan-derived ten-step arc is a **narrative engine**, and importing narrative into a store is a category error. Stores sell states, not character development. The extractable ontology is one sentence: *settlement does not reverse what has been released into the world.*
2. **Philosophy disguised as merchandise?** Candidates A, B, and E are at risk of exactly this. Candidate C is not. The difference is instructive: C converts the idea into a commercial instrument with a spec table; the others state the idea.
3. **Multiple strong products?** Yes — but only via the narrow extract, and only through **commercial instruments that natively encode it**: settlements, write-offs, liens, escrow, non-refundables, liability caps, statutes of limitation, goodwill credits. This register is rich enough for many products.
4. **Commerce transforms or illustrates?** The narrow version transforms — liability caps *are* finite responsibility in native commercial grammar; the store already speaks this language. The broad version illustrates.
5. **Reopens sentimentality?** Yes if the feeling leads; no if the ledger leads. Entry path must be through instruments, never through the harmed party's interior.
6. **Moral seriousness without didacticism?** Only under one condition: **the store never takes the moral position.** The store must remain the indifferent party for whom the matter is closed because payment cleared. The disturbance comes from the system's closure, not from commentary. The moment the store mourns, the work becomes a pamphlet.

**Structural discovery the team has missed:** the store is currently dyadic — buyer and system only. Moral residue is inherently third-party (the claimant, the creditor, the one harmed). Without a third party the territory collapses into consumer regret, which the store already has. Commerce happens to have native third-party instruments — liens, settlements, escrow, gift receipts, payees — so the territory *can* work, but only through those instruments. This should be a design constraint, not a suggestion.

**Verdict: TEST ONLY**, narrowed and renamed. "Moral Residue" names a feeling; the workable territory names a ledger state. Call it **Unsettled Consequence** or **Settlement Failure**. Two to three SKUs maximum, instrument-shaped.

## 10. Candidate A — What Payment Cannot Undo

- **Product strength:** The underlying collision (Commerce treats payment as closure / some consequences are not reversible by exchange) is genuinely strong.
- **Commerce necessity:** Currently low. As titled, nothing in the transaction behaves differently. It is Commerce-Displayed by a store doing no display work.
- **Accessibility:** Moderate — the title is comprehensible but reads as a thesis.
- **Freshness:** Weak. The title collides with a famous, exactly-on-topic book — Michael Sandel's *What Money Can't Buy: The Moral Limits of Markets*. Any reader who knows the book will read the product as commentary on it.
- **Naming quality:** Poor by the project's own best practice. `Yesterday` does not explain itself; this title is a wall label. "What X Cannot Y" is also an exhausted poetic template.
- **Scalability as a Grammar seed:** The concept scales; the title does not.

**Better names** (instrument-shaped, no thesis): `Paid in Full` · `Settled in Full` · `Balance: $0.00` · or the document itself, `Settlement — No Admission of Fault`. The strongest version is a receipt-shaped artifact where the words "paid in full" and the continuing existence of the thing do all the work.

**REWORK** — keep the collision, discard the title, re-instantiate as a commercial instrument.

---

## 11. Candidate B — Self-Assigned Responsibility

This is a category label wearing a product's clothes. `Assigned by: Self / Scope: Expanded / Control: Limited` is a spec sheet for a psychological posture. Nothing is exchanged, owned, delivered, or recorded. Commerce does nothing — this is Commerce-Displayed with zero display work, violating the team's own minority rule. **DROP.**

Salvageable derivation from the same ontology, via the insurance register (the native system for responsibility-beyond-control): a policy document reading `Voluntary Liability Coverage — In Force · Covered Events: None · Premium: $6.00/yr`. The customer purchases standing liability for events that are never enumerated. Even this risks overlapping with Candidate E; treat as optional.

---

## 12. Candidate C — Repair Scope: Partial

The strongest of the five. It is concrete, instrument-shaped, and it alone converts "finite responsibility" from a posture into an object-state. It gives Commerce real, unresolved work: What does the customer physically receive — a repair order? Who selects the repairable 18%? (It should be the store — the system choosing the fraction is where the disturbance lives.) Is the order ever marked complete — and if yes, at 18%, the record itself becomes the artwork. Return policy: the unrepaired remainder is non-returnable, obviously.

Risk: it reads as pure administrative metaphor unless an inferable human situation sits behind the numbers. Keep the referent implicit but discoverable. **KEEP.**

---

## 13. Candidate D — Distance to Home: 1,847 mi

Apply the prompt's own test: what does Commerce actually do to it? Currently, nothing. The number performs the same precision-as-affect function as `17,400 Hz`, the name is a poetry line with a unit attached, and homesickness is the *least* fresh emotional territory in the project — this is the v0.1 sentimental register re-entering through a back door. As a store feature it is invisible (real stores already compute distance to the default address). Salvage paths exist (shipping priced per mile, deliverable to one address only, ever) but they repair a product whose core emotion is conventional. **DROP from v0.6.**

---

## 14. Candidate E — Control Over Final Outcome — Unavailable

1. **Systemic mechanic vs SKU:** Clearly stronger as a mechanic. Complete outcome control is a property of the *store's offer structure*, not of any one object.
2. **Too explicit?** Yes, in current wording — "control over final outcome" is the thesis in plain words. Quiet versions: a checkout add-on labeled `Outcome Guarantee` that is permanently greyed out; a warranty clause reading `Coverage of final outcome: not included`; best of all, a storefront filter — `Products with guaranteed outcomes: (0 results)` — which shows the system *failing to provide what everyone wants* without naming it.
3. **Repetition:** Becomes a gag after the third appearance. Hard cap: two placements, non-adjacent.
4. **Strongest non-product intervention?** Yes — alongside the zero-result filter, this is among the strongest non-SKU moves available. **REWORK as mechanic, not SKU.**

## 16. New Instances — Unsettled Consequence Territory

1. **`Minimum Payment`** — An installment plan on an outstanding balance whose principal does not decrease. *Commerce:* recurring billing; order history grows monthly while "Balance Remaining" never moves; canceling the subscription does not discharge the balance. *Risk:* reads as a credit-card joke unless the referent debt is inferable and the store processes the subscription with total normality.

2. **`Settlement — No Admission of Fault`** — Compensation issued together with the explicit record that nothing is admitted. *Commerce:* the receipt and the waiver are one artifact; the ledger shows both `PAID` and `NO FAULT` on adjacent lines; returning the settlement voids it. *Risk:* legal register too heavy if it drifts law-firm-shaped instead of store-shaped.

3. **`Uncollectible`** — An obligation the system has written off: it cannot be paid and cannot be forgiven, only abandoned. *Commerce:* priced at $0.00 (book value of a write-off) yet persists permanently on the account; payment attempts return "payment not accepted." *Risk:* $0.00 pricing is a known conceptual-art move; the account-history persistence must carry it.

4. **`Goodwill Credit — $5.00`** — Compensation issued as a gesture rather than an obligation, and therefore not usable for what it was issued for. *Commerce:* a fully functioning store credit, expiring in 90 days, category-restricted from the item the loss concerned. *Risk:* cute unless the restriction is enforced with complete straightness.

5. **`Conveys With Prior Claim`** — Property whose transfer does not interrupt someone else's claim on it; you can own it, they still hold it. *Commerce:* title transfers normally; the claim line item migrates to the new owner's account; resale transfers the claim with it. *Risk:* register opacity — liens are not mass-familiar paperwork.

6. **`Limitations Period — Lapsed`** — The right to file a claim that expired on schedule; the damage remains, the complaint route closed. *Commerce:* priced by remaining window, decaying to $0.00 at expiry; after expiry the product is free, non-returnable, and — crucially — **non-reviewable**, because the review channel *is* the claim channel. *Risk:* deadline gimmick unless the closed-review state registers.

7. **`In Escrow, Indefinitely`** — Payment held for a transaction that completes when a specified event occurs; the event is not forthcoming. *Commerce:* the balance is visible in the account, tagged "held," unusable and unreleasable; support can confirm it, not release it. *Risk:* requires real account behavior; dead on arrival if checkout is theatrical.

8. **`Release of Claims, Retroactive`** — The customer pays to certify that the store owes them nothing for what has already occurred. *Commerce:* a signed release as a required checkout step; the account thereafter displays "No claims available." *Risk:* the most didactic of the set; borders dark-pattern-as-art, which may be the point but must be watched.

All eight: commerce-native, concrete, no abstract moral nouns, no therapy language, none reproducible in a poetry book.

## 17. Product Behavior Sheet — Evaluation

**Essential:** Name · Commerce Type · **Unit Definition** (missing — add) · Price + price basis · **Lifecycle Deviations** · **Failure Test** (the single best instrument in the method — keep).

**Bureaucracy:** Order Confirmation / Fulfillment / Delivery as three separate fields (90% of entries will read "standard") — collapse into one Deviations field. `Resale if relevant` — conditional fields are not fields. `Representation logic` — undefined term; define or cut. **Product Grammar** — the v0.4 criticism called the taxonomy over-segmented; the criticism was never answered, and the sheet quietly re-inherits the taxonomy. Cut Grammar as a mandatory field; the Failure Test already performs the sorting function better.

**Missing fields:**
- **Unit Definition** (as above).
- **The Gap:** what the customer believes they bought vs. what the system records. This is the artwork's atomic move; it should be one mandatory sentence.
- **Third Party:** does anyone besides buyer and store exist in this product — claimant, recipient, beneficiary, payee?
- **Complaint Posture:** what does support say when the customer asks the obvious question ("where is my Yesterday?").

**Structural rule:** write the store's *default policy document once* (standard confirmation, standard fulfillment, standard return window), then each sheet records only **deviations from default**. Full 17-field sheets for the 3–4 flagship SKUs only; the rest get a six-line card. A sheet where most fields say "standard" is paperwork, and the R&D system must not outgrow the artwork.

## 18. Adversarial Critique

1. **The project is overfitting to critic feedback.** Four evaluations, four correction sets, pendulum managed by rules. The visitor will never say "Commerce-Displayed products must earn their place." Designing against reviewers produces work that satisfies reviewers.
2. **The vocabulary has outgrown the artwork.** Product Grammar, Product Ontology, Commerce Type, Register Fidelity, Progressive Displacement, Residue scores — the taxonomy is now larger than the Catalog, and at least one scored metric is undefined.
3. **The lifecycle is becoming the artwork.** If the most interesting thing in OTHER GOODS is what order history says six months later, the products are props for a discoverable gimmick. A store where every product has an engineered temporal twist is as formulaic as a store where every product is a sad pun.
4. **The naming is still one formula:** [system-register noun phrase] + [number with unit]. `Yesterday` fits it, `17,400 Hz` fits it, `Distance to Home: 1,847 mi` fits it. Deepening the sheets does not change the formula; it adds spreadsheet columns around it.
5. **Emotional immediacy is draining.** `Yesterday` and `First Typo` hit in under a second with zero domain knowledge. `Repair Scope: Partial` requires ten seconds and a spec table. Register Fidelity systematically trades universality for insider texture. Each v0.6 flagship should pass a one-second test: a cold visitor is disturbed by the product page alone.
6. **The store is dyadic in a territory that requires third parties.** No gifting, no recipients, no claimants anywhere in the infrastructure. This is a structural gap that Moral Residue exposes but cannot fix from inside.
7. **The Nolan material is second-hand and narrative-shaped.** A commentator's interpretation of an auteur's arc, filtered through the team, is three steps from its source; and a ten-step character structure is the wrong shape for merchandise. The single usable sentence should be extracted and the rest discarded.
8. **Realism paradox.** Deepening 12–16 products makes the store *more* curated and *less* like commerce. Credible infrastructure is mostly boring. If v0.6 produces no default-behavior layer, scaling to 100–200 SKUs becomes economically impossible and the project will remain a fourteen-object gallery forever.
9. **No falsification channel exists.** One observed user session — watching a real person decide whether to checkout on `Yesterday` — would falsify or confirm more than four written evaluations. There is no excuse for its absence at this stage.

## 19. Forced Decisions

**A. Reduce to 12–16 and deepen?** **YES** — as R&D only, with mandatory output of reusable mechanics plus a default-policy layer; 16 bespoke one-offs would be a failure mode.

**B. Full lifecycle as core criterion?** **ONLY FOR SOME PRODUCT TYPES** — the three-timestamp question is mandatory for all; post-purchase design is mandatory only where the system's record diverges from the customer's belief.

**C. Price Logic mandatory?** **ONLY FOR SOME PRODUCT TYPES** — Unit Definition mandatory for all; price differentiation mandatory for Commerce-Generated/Activated; "fixed, standard" is a valid answer elsewhere.

**D. Inventory Logic mandatory?** **ONLY FOR SOME PRODUCT TYPES** — mandatory where scarcity, regeneration, or rivalry is constitutive (`First Canceled Order`: qty 1; `Yesterday`: daily restock at midnight — the project's best undiscovered mechanic).

**E. Register Fidelity stronger than Dryness?** **YES** — with the mass-familiarity constraint, with the rule governing paperwork more than titles, and with occasional total-mundanity inversion permitted.

**F. Moral Residue into v0.6?** **TEST ONLY** — narrowed to Unsettled Consequence, 2–3 SKUs, instrument-shaped, ledger-led, third-party-instrument required, store never takes the moral position.

**G. What Payment Cannot Undo?** **REWORK** — keep the collision, discard the title (Sandel collision), re-instantiate as `Paid in Full` / `Settled in Full` / `Settlement — No Admission of Fault`.

**H. Definitely NOT in v0.6:** **Self-Assigned Responsibility** — a category label with no transaction, no commerce function, and no path to repair.

**I. Single most important unresolved question:** **Is the unit of the work the product or the transaction?** Equivalently: is the artwork a gallery of strange titles, or a machine that irreversibly changes state when used — and does real money change hands? Every hypothesis in this document is ambiguous on this. Product-centered design deepens titles; transaction-centered design deepens the moment of exchange and everything after. These are two different artworks, they require different v0.6 structures, and no Product Behavior Sheet should be written until this is decided.

## Overall Verdict

**READY WITH ONE REQUIRED CORRECTION**

The correction: before any Product Behavior Sheets are written, define the artwork's atomic unit — the minimal complete experience, from product page through recorded transaction — and decide whether real exchange occurs. Derive the sheet fields from that definition (deviation-based, with Unit Definition, The Gap, Third Party, and Complaint Posture added), rather than from a checklist. Everything else in the current direction — reduction, lifecycle pressure, Register Fidelity, a narrowed Unsettled Consequence territory — is fit to proceed.

## Strongest Current Insight

Section 7's reframe: one uniform, indifferent commercial system processing objects of radically different temperatures. All variance belongs to the objects; none to the system. The system must never acknowledge the differential — uniformity is the pressure that makes the objects legible.

## Biggest Overcorrection Risk

The critic-feedback loop: rules correcting the last error rather than the next, a taxonomy larger than the Catalog, and undefined metrics (e.g., "Residue") being scored as if they were measurements. Process is replacing judgment.

## View on Register Fidelity

Stronger than Dryness because it explains Dryness's successes rather than copying them. Adopt with three constraints: registers must be mass-familiar; the rule governs system paperwork more than titles; and it will become a device within three pages unless offset by occasional total mundanity — a product named like a real SKU is now the project's most radical naming move.

## View on Moral Residue

A genuine but narrow addition. The ten-step arc is narrative, not ontology; the extractable ontology is one sentence — settlement does not reverse consequence. It enters v0.6 only through commercial instruments (settlements, write-offs, liens, escrow, limitation periods, goodwill credits), only through third-party structures, and only if the store never takes the moral position: the system's indifference is the artwork, its closure is the disturbance.

## Best New Candidate

**`Minimum Payment`** — recurring billing against a principal that never decreases. Universal comprehension, pure commerce-native grammar, and Commerce does everything: billing, order history growth, non-discharge on cancellation. It outranks all five candidates under review, including the strongest of them, `Repair Scope: Partial`.

## Weakest New Candidate

**`Self-Assigned Responsibility`** — a psychological category label with no exchange, no record, and no commerce function; it fails the project's own Commerce-Displayed minority rule.

## One Product You Would Add

**`Gift Receipt`** — included with any gift purchase; the recipient may return the item for store credit, and the giver is never notified. It introduces the third party the entire infrastructure lacks, uses commerce's native instrument for knowledge asymmetry, and carries quiet emotional weight without a single sentimental word.

## One Methodological Rule You Would Remove

**Product Grammar as a mandatory classification field.** The over-segmentation criticism of v0.4 was never resolved, and the behavior sheet silently re-inherits the taxonomy. The Failure Test ("what disappears if Commerce is removed?") already performs the sorting function better. Keep Grammar as an optional tag at most.

## One Thing That Must Be Decided Before v0.6

**Product or transaction — which is the unit of the artwork, and is the exchange real?** Until this is answered, the post-purchase hypothesis, the Behavior Sheet, and the Moral Residue territory are all being designed against different, unstated assumptions.

# External Evaluation — Commerce R&D Collection v0.5

---

## A. Overall Direction

**Verdict: WORKING BUT NEEDS CORRECTION**

v0.5 solved v0.4's breadth problem but not its depth problem. The collection now spans nostalgia, perception, logistics, institutions, and industry — the register range is genuinely wide, and several new products (17,400 Hz; Unit 12B; First Canceled Order) prove the system can leave the "poetic memory shop" behind. But the widened range exposes two problems that were survivable at 24 SKUs and fatal at 200: **redundancy** (roughly a third of the products are variants of the same grammar — the "irreversible personal moment" family is still overpopulated), and **mechanical shallowness** (commerce is still mostly a costume: product pages exist, but prices, inventory behavior, and post-purchase relationships remain theoretical). The needed correction has shifted from *what the products are* to *what the store does*.

## 9. English Product Naming Review

### Strong English Name — keep as written

- **Yesterday** — flawless: one word, dry, native, and the concept is fully inside it.
- **17,400 Hz** — the store's best naming decision; indistinguishable from a real test-tone listing.
- **First Canceled Order** — clean, system-native.
- **First Typo in a New Notebook** — natural and precise; "new" carries the ontology.
- **The Room Before It Became Home** — natural spoken English; the warmth is in the phenomenon, not the wording.
- **$8.00 Price Drop, 10 Minutes After Purchase** — reads like a real notification subject line; perfectly e-commerce-native.
- **Unit 12B, Unoccupied** — the flat cadence of records; correct.
- **12 Stairs, Never Counted** / **30th Rain Through the Same Window** / **2 Hours from a Canceled Appointment** / **Before Reading the Reviews** — all acceptable, natural, correctly weighted.
- **3 Seconds of Ocean** — clean and graspable; the poetic register is a known risk but tolerable because the *unit* is the concept.

### Strong Concept, Name Needs Revision

- **Summer, Age 16** — the comma construction reads translated. The concept ("varies by customer") should live in a spec field, not the title. Options: *Summer, Age 16 — Varies by Customer* / *Summer (Age 16)* / *Your Sixteenth Summer* (most natural English, most sentimental — acceptable if used knowingly).
- **Last 10 Minutes Before Knowing the Ending** — grammatically tangled; unclear whether it means the film's final ten minutes or the ten minutes before being told the ending. Options: *The Last Ten Minutes, Unspoiled* / *The Last Ten Minutes, First Time* / *Final Ten Minutes, First Viewing*.
- **Day After the Return Window Closed** — tense is unstable. Strongest option: ***Return Window Closed*** — it is a literal system string that already appears in real order histories; naming the product after it is maximal realism. Alternatives: *The Day After the Return Window Closes* / *One Day Past Return*.
- **Upper-Right Corner, Dented in Transit** — the comma splice feels translated. Drop the comma: *Upper-Right Corner Dented in Transit* / *Dent, Upper-Right, Sustained in Transit*.
- **Rear View of a Building, Bus Window Only** — "Bus Window Only" is not idiomatic English. Options: *Rear of the Building, Route 47, Window Seat* (specificity = realism) / *The Back of a Building, Seen from a Bus* / *A Building's Rear, from the Bus Window*.
- **One Day of the Trip, No Photos** — borderline acceptable; the trip context is ontologically necessary (see §10). Sharper option: *Day 3: No Photos* — mimics the exact format where the absence would appear. Or keep current and let "Photos Taken: 0" live in the specs.
- **Residual Warmth on a Park Bench** — revert. See §10. Options: *Warmth Left on a Park Bench* (original) / ***Warm Spot on a Park Bench*** (the actual folk term — most natural of all).
- **First 12 Sheets Discarded Before Printing** — factually muddled: the sheets *are* printed, during setup. Options: *First 12 Calibration Sheets* / *12 Sheets, Discarded in Setup* / *Makeready Sheets, First 12* ("makeready" is the real industry term and grounds the product in actual production).
- **Wrong-Turn Alley** (if the grammar survives in another host) — *The Alley from the Wrong Turn* / *Alley, Found by Mistake*.

### Name Hides a Weak Concept

- **Wrong-Turn Alley** — the name is charming (problematically so — it sounds like a fantasy location); the product beneath has no commerce traction.
- **"These Days," 5 Years Ago** — clever indexical wordplay masking the fact that it is a weaker duplicate of Yesterday.
- **Last 10 Minutes Before Knowing the Ending** — the title performs suspense the concept doesn't earn; beneath the drama is a familiar spoiler-state.

## 10. The "Dryness" Question

The principle is half-right and currently misapplied, because it conflates **decoration** with **image**.

- "Residual Warmth on a Park Bench" is a *worse* name than "Warmth Left on a Park Bench." "Left" is not sentimental decoration — it carries the ontology (someone was here; the trace is theirs; it is running out). "Residual" is register anxiety: pseudo-forensic vocabulary that removes naturalness without removing sentiment, because the sentiment lives in the *phenomenon* (a stranger's body heat), which no adjective can dry out. "Residual warmth" is itself a technical cliché — the revision swapped one sentimental register (tender) for another (clinical).
- The trip example proves the other half: word count is not the axis. "Of the Trip" is ontology, not decoration — cut it and the absence becomes invisible, because unphotographed days are only remarkable against the expectation structure of travel.

**Proposed replacement principle — Register Matching:**

> Name each product in the language of the real-world system that would plausibly handle it. Where no such system exists, use plain spoken English. Dryness is a byproduct of register fidelity, not a goal.

A return-window product should sound like a policy string ("Return Window Closed"). A frequency product should sound like a test-tone listing ("17,400 Hz"). A calibration product should sound like a production line-item ("Makeready Sheets, First 12"). A park bench has no native commercial register — so plain, warm English is correct, and "Warm Spot on a Park Bench" is the right name.

**Warning:** dryness can become a house style. Three v0.5 titles already use the "Noun, Status" template (Unit 12B, Unoccupied / 12 Stairs, Never Counted / a possible "Ocean, 3 Seconds"). It is a strong template; at 200 SKUs it would become the new sentimentality — the sentimentality of bureaucratic cool. Real stores mix marketing names, spec names, status strings, and plain names. **Register diversity is itself a realism signal.**

## 11. Sentimental Bias Check

**Still feeding the "sentimental shop" reading:** 01 (the largest contributor), 03, 12, 13, 19, 20, 21 — plus 11's whimsy. That is 8/24 ≈ 33%, down from v0.4, but *concentrated in one grammar family* (irreversible personal moments), so they now read as redundancy rather than range.

**Actively breaking the reading:** 05, 07, 09 (commerce-native shocks), 22 (perception), 23 (institution), 24 (industry), 08 (logistics), 17 (counting), and 15 partially (bodily rather than emotional).

**Is the Collection diverse enough for Progressive Displacement?** Register-wise, yes — the span from "Summer, Age 16" to "17,400 Hz" is genuinely wide. Structurally, not yet: the collection is organized as *poles* (tender-personal ↔ cold-institutional) with a thin middle. The middle register — ordinary, neither tender nor technical (14, 17 occupy it) — is what makes a store feel like a store rather than a designed contrast. Cut within the sentimental family; do not keep balancing by adding cold products. And note the new risk: warm/cold alternation can itself become the displacement formula (see §19).

## 12. Commerce-Native vs. Ontology-First

**Commerce-Generated** (concept barely exists without commerce): 05, 06, 07, 09 — 4 products (≈17%).

**Commerce-Activated** (independent ontology, substantially transformed): 02, 04, 08, 10, 12 (weakly — via photo-book delivery), 15, 17, 18 (weakly), 20, 22, 23, 24 — 12 products (≈50%).

**Commerce-Displayed** (concept mostly complete already; commerce adds little): 01 (as currently conceived), 03, 11, 13, 14, 16, 19, 21 — 8 products (≈33%).

**Answers:**

1. Current balance: roughly 17 / 50 / 33.
2. Not healthy. The Displayed third is too large, and half of it is redundant family variants.
3. Recommended: ~25% Generated / ~50% Activated / ~20–25% Displayed. Generated products are the store's *spine* (they prove commerce is constitutive, not a costume); Activated is the workhorse; Displayed is *texture* and must earn its slots through exceptional residue.
4. Displayed products worth keeping: **19** (residue justifies it; the human anchor), **14** (counterweight grammar), **01** *if* reviews are built to instantiate it (which would migrate it to Activated).
5. Remove: **21** (derivative), **13** (redundant + flagged territory), **11** (no traction), **16** (grammar survives elsewhere), **03** (unless rebuilt around condition grading).

**On the unresolved theory question:** the previous evaluator's challenge is correct, and v0.5 quietly proves it — the best new products (05, 07, 09, and partially 08) mine ontologies that commerce itself produces: canceled orders, expired rights, post-purchase revaluation, transit damage. The dichotomy is false. Commerce-generated products *are* ontology-first products whose ontology happens to be commercial. The instruction should be: mine commerce's own ontologies *and* collide commerce with non-commercial ontologies — but in both cases, a commerce mechanism must do conceptual work (see §19).

- Generative? Yes: *Floor 13, Not Built* / *Platform 4, Never Constructed* / *Serial #4118, Skipped*.

## 15. Missing Product Territories

1. **Logistics limbo** — *Lost in Transit* (or *Last Scan: Memphis, 11 Days Ago*). Parcels genuinely exist in this state; unclaimed freight is really auctioned. Commerce-native and eerie.
2. **Unused infrastructure capacity** — *Dark Fiber, 2.3 km, Unlit*. Dark fiber is a real, leaseable commodity that carries nothing. Perfect ontology, perfect realism anchor.
3. **Legal fictions** — *Right of Way, Never Exercised*. Law produces objects with no matter; an unused easement is a power that never fired.
4. **Metrology / standards** — *The Retired Kilogram*. The physical artifact that defined the kilogram until 2019 still exists in a vault, institutionally unemployed. Physically unchanged, ontologically retired — the project's whole thesis in one object.
5. **Machine states** — *Unresolved Sync Conflict*. Two versions of the same file, both real, both claiming truth.
6. **Standby objects** — *The Emergency Exit, Never Opened*. Objects whose function is to never be used; inspected, maintained, unexercised.
7. **Maintenance states** — *Awaiting Parts*. A real repair status; a machine in suspended animation.

Pattern worth adopting: most of these already have real commercial or administrative language attached (claim categories, service statuses, lease terms) — the strongest future products will borrow native registers rather than invent them.

## 16. Post-Purchase Test (8 strongest: 02, 04, 05, 07, 08, 15, 22, 23)

**Yesterday**
- Confirmation: an email timestamped the morning after; "Your Yesterday has been confirmed."
- Delivery: instant — a dated certificate; the date is the product.
- Six months: order history reads "Yesterday — ordered March 3" — now doubly false; overwritten ~183 times.
- Return/repurchase: return window structurally always closed; repurchase tomorrow yields the same SKU, a different referent. Subscription option ("Yesterday, daily") is the killer feature.

**First Typo in a New Notebook**
- Confirmation: bought a condition, shipped inside a notebook.
- Delivery: sealed notebook + certificate "typo pending" — the product completes itself on the customer's first error.
- Six months: "Delivered — condition realized (typo, p.1)."
- Return/repurchase: post-typo return = returning a used condition; resale listings distinguish "authentic typo" from "unused, typo pending."

**First Canceled Order**
- Confirmation: the confirmation *is* the cancellation — "Order canceled. Thank you for your purchase."
- Delivery: nothing ships; the cancellation is the fulfillment; status reads "Canceled (Complete)."
- Six months: a canceled order that also shows as purchased and fulfilled.
- Return/repurchase: repurchasing produces a *different* product — "Second Canceled Order," cheaper.

**Day After the Return Window Closed**
- Confirmation: delivery scheduled for the day after the customer's own window closes.
- Delivery: an email on day 31: "Your right to return this has expired. Delivery complete."
- Six months: "Delivered — ownership now irreversible."
- Return/repurchase: the return interface exists and always rejects; the rejection message is the product's content.

**Upper-Right Corner, Dented in Transit**
- Confirmation: "Ships in packaging designed to reproduce the damage."
- Delivery: box arrives dented upper-right; QA certifies dent authenticity.
- Six months: "Delivered (dented as ordered)."
- Return/repurchase: replacement triggers a warning — "Replacement will destroy the item you purchased" — refund means surrendering the dent.

**Warmth on a Park Bench**
- Confirmation: coordinates, live temperature, decay rate, pickup window.
- Delivery: pickup only — shipping would cool it.
- Six months: "Collected, 29.4°C at acquisition" or "Expired — not collected."
- Return/repurchase: no returns (condition decays); every bench and every prior sitter is a new batch.

**17,400 Hz**
- Confirmation: download link + spec sheet; "audibility not guaranteed."
- Delivery: 30-second WAV, instant — the most realistic delivery in the store.
- Six months: "Downloaded twice."
- Return/repurchase: return reason "I couldn't hear it" — the return flow itself is the thesis. Reviews effectively disclose reviewer age.

**Unit 12B, Unoccupied**
- Confirmation: vacancy record + occupancy monitoring.
- Delivery: floor plan, mailbox photo, certification of current emptiness.
- Six months: "Status: OCCUPIED since October 12 — your copy covers the unoccupied period only." The product spoils by third-party action.
- Return/repurchase: no returns; repurchase means a different unit elsewhere.

**Should Post-Purchase Behavior become a mandatory R&D field?**

**YES** — as a mandatory *test*, not a mandatory *feature*. Every product must answer the four questions at R&D stage. If the honest answer is "nothing meaningful happens after payment," that is diagnostic: the product is Commerce-Displayed, and the catalog must ration those. The test doesn't force mechanics; it forces honesty about which products have earned the store. Note that all eight above survive the test — and that 09 (Price Drop), despite strong commerce-nativity, is the one that does not obviously deepen after purchase, which is exactly why it sits outside the top eight.

## 19. Adversarial Critique

**The generative formula is already visible, and it has no rejection criterion.** [Article/ordinal] + [ordinary noun] + [condition qualifier] produces a plausible OTHER GOODS title for almost anything: "The Third Yawn of the Meeting," "One Sock, Lost in the Wash." Both instantly "qualify." A grammar that accepts everything generates slogans, not products. The missing instrument is a falsifiable test: *does a commerce mechanism — price logic, policy, inventory behavior, review semantics, delivery — do conceptual work, changing what the thing means?* A spec field reading "Measurable: Yes" is a caption, not a collision. Half of v0.5 passes only the caption test.

**Naming is doing ontology's job.** v0.5's improvement is largely lexical. "12 Stairs, Never Counted" is a title plus a hope; if the store cannot answer what the customer *has* after purchase, the product is a caption in a catalog, and 200 captions is a poetry book with a shopping cart. The post-purchase blind spot is now acknowledged in theory but remains unimplemented in practice — the R&D descriptions still describe product pages, not purchase relationships.

**Sentimentality was repressed, not cured.** The correction added cold objects rather than discriminating among warm ones. The result risks a new formula: sentimental↔bureaucratic oscillation as the displacement mechanism. The actual lesson of v0.4 was never "emotion is bad" — "The Room Before It Became Home" is sentimental and excellent; "A Message Regretted Immediately After Sending" was sentimental and weak. The difference is whether commerce bites. v0.4's weak products failed because commerce had nothing to do, not because they were tender.

**The store has product pages but no market.** Prices are nowhere discussed as a conceptual surface. A real store's realism lives in its numbers: why does Yesterday cost what it costs, why is Warmth priced per remaining degree, why is 17,400 Hz $0.99 like an actual test tone? Arbitrary prices will read as arbitrary at catalog scale. Relatedly, at ~30 products the visitor's pattern recognition saturates; at 200, clever titles exhaust and only *system behavior* (reviews that argue, inventory that misbehaves, order history that lies) sustains interest.

**A meta-risk:** the project's evaluation apparatus (versioned taxonomies, grammar consolidation, external critique rounds) can become the real artwork, with the store as its illustration. The taxonomy is scaffolding; visitors will only ever meet the storefront.

**Three problems to solve before scaling:**
1. **Define and apply the commerce-work test** — a rejection criterion distinguishing products where commerce does conceptual work from products wearing a commercial costume.
2. **Implement the purchase relationship** for the strongest products — confirmation, order history, returns — so depth exists in behavior, not only in product-page copy.
3. **Build the price and inventory logic** — a coherent internal market, because price is the store's most confident commercial signal and is currently its least examined conceptual surface.

## Biggest Improvement from v0.4
The expansion into non-sentimental ontologies with real commercial adjacency — Transaction Artifacts, Instrument-Only Objects, Institutional Phantoms. The store now has register range and, in 17,400 Hz, a proof that it can be indistinguishable from real commerce while selling the imperceptible.

## Biggest Remaining Weakness
The store is still a catalog of titles. For roughly half the collection, commerce is display rather than machinery, and the purchase relationship — the layer where e-commerce is most powerful — remains unimplemented in practice.

## Strongest Product in v0.5
**Yesterday.** The cleanest ontology fused with the deepest commerce collision: it corrupts SKU identity, order history, receipts, and repurchase simultaneously, and it keeps working after the visitor leaves the site.

## Weakest Product in v0.5
**"These Days," 5 Years Ago.** A derivative of the collection's own best product, dependent on quotation marks to explain itself, adding no new commerce surface.

## Best Naming Decision
**17,400 Hz** — it proves the register-matching principle: a name borrowed from a real product category, doing the concept's work with zero poetry.

## Worst Naming Decision
**"Residual Warmth on a Park Bench."** Dryness applied as lexical substitution: it removes naturalness without removing sentiment and swaps a tender cliché for a clinical one. Revert, or use "Warm Spot on a Park Bench."

## Most Valuable New Grammar Territory
**Instrument-Only Objects** — measurable-but-imperceptible phenomena. Most generative, most realism-anchored, and the direct fulfillment of the convergence between the previous evaluators.

## Most Dangerous Blind Spot
**Price.** The entire R&D treats price as a display field. Nowhere is it discussed as conceptual surface — yet it is the store's most confident commercial signal and, at catalog scale, the first place arbitrary thinking will show.

## One Thing to Do Before v0.6
Wire the complete transaction loop for the eight strongest products — real prices, confirmation emails, an order-history view simulated six months forward, and working return flows — and let the mechanics, not the titles, decide which products survive.

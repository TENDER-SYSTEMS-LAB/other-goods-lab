**A. Overall Direction**

**WORKING BUT NEEDS CORRECTION**

The collection has successfully reduced the pure nostalgia/relationship concentration of v0.4 and introduced several stronger commerce-native and institutional objects. However, the generative formula remains too visible: too many products still follow the pattern “ordinary phenomenon + artificial boundary/unit/condition = product.” Progressive Displacement is only partially achieved; several entries still read as literary captions rather than merchandise that survives contact with cart, fulfillment, and order history.

**10. The “Dryness” Question**

The principle is useful but incomplete. Dryness successfully removes perfume-language from “Warmth Left…” → “Residual Warmth…”, improving commercial tone without killing the image. However, pure dryness can become its own aesthetic formula (“Residual X on Y”, “Nth Z Never Counted”).  

Better principle:  
**Use the driest name that still forces the essential commercial collision into view.**  
If the collision requires a human or contextual cue (travel expectation, return window, price timing), retain the minimal cue. Context is not sentiment; it is the condition that makes the product purchasable rather than merely nameable.

**11. Sentimental Bias Check**

Still increases the sentimental reading: Summer Age 16, One Day of the Trip No Photos, The Room Before It Became Home, 6-Minute Unrecorded Conversation, Wrong-Turn Alley.  

Actively breaks it: 17,400 Hz, Unit 12B Unoccupied, First 12 Sheets Discarded, Day After the Return Window Closed, Upper-Right Corner Dented, First Canceled Order, $8.00 Price Drop.  

The Collection is closer to emotional diversity but not yet sufficient for reliable Progressive Displacement. Too many remaining soft entries allow a visitor to re-collapse the store into “quiet moments and absences.”

**12. Commerce-Native vs. Ontology-First**

**Commerce-Generated**: 05 First Canceled Order, 07 Day After Return Window, 09 $8.00 Price Drop.  

**Commerce-Activated**: 02 Yesterday, 04 First Typo, 08 Upper-Right Corner, 14 30th Rain, 15 Residual Warmth, 20 2 Hours from Canceled Appointment, 22 17,400 Hz, 23 Unit 12B, 24 First 12 Sheets.  

**Commerce-Displayed**: 01, 03, 06, 10, 11, 12, 13, 16, 17, 18, 19, 21.

Current balance ≈ 12 % generated / 40 % activated / 48 % displayed. Mildly unhealthy; too many Displayed items dilute the store’s formal power. Recommended target for a mature catalog: 20–25 % Generated, 45–50 % Activated, ≤30 % Displayed.  

Worth keeping among Displayed: 17 12 Stairs, 21 These Days, 14 30th Rain (as counterweight). Remove or heavily rework: 11, 13, 16, 01, 06.

**14. Grammar Consolidation**

1. **Temporal Drift** — Referent changes while name stays fixed. Examples: Yesterday; “These Days,” 5 Years Ago. New: “Next Week, Last Year”.  
2. **Condition Collapse** — Acquisition or use destroys the advertised state. Examples: First Typo; Last 10 Minutes Before Knowing. New: “Seal Intact Until Opened”.  
3. **Commerce-Native State** — Object exists only through commercial procedure. Examples: First Canceled Order; Day After Return Window; $8 Price Drop. New: “Order Placed During System Outage”.  
4. **Institutional Absence** — Administratively real, physically empty or null. Examples: Unit 12B Unoccupied. New: “Reserved Seat, Never Claimed”.  
5. **Threshold Perception** — Measurable yet near or beyond ordinary senses. Examples: 17,400 Hz. New: “0.3 °C Below Detectable Warmth”.  
6. **Process Byproduct** — Exists only as side-effect of producing something else. Examples: First 12 Sheets Discarded. New: “Test Print, Run 47”.  
7. **Artificial Unit** — Continuous phenomenon forced into discrete commercial unit. Examples: 3 Seconds of Ocean; 12 Stairs. New: “One Breath of City Air”.  
8. **Defect as Identity** — Damage or irregularity is the SKU. Examples: Upper-Right Corner Dented. New: “Label Misapplied, 2 mm Offset”.

**16. Post-Purchase Test (Top 8)**

02 Yesterday  
Confirmation: “Yesterday”. Delivery: nothing physical; status updates daily. History after 6 months: still listed as “Yesterday” (now false). Return: impossible; repurchase buys a new Yesterday.

04 First Typo  
Confirmation: new notebook state. Delivery: physical notebook + digital condition note. History: “First Typo (Occurred)”. Return of used notebook invalidates original SKU.

07 Day After Return Window  
Confirmation: expired right. Delivery: status change only. History: permanent record of closed window. Return attempt: system rejects as already expired.

08 Upper-Right Corner Dented  
Confirmation: specific damage. Delivery: dented object. History: “Dented in Transit – Upper Right”. Return of undamaged version destroys identity.

05 First Canceled Order  
Confirmation: the cancellation itself. Delivery: cancellation confirmation is the product. History: “First Canceled Order – Fulfilled by Cancellation”.

22 17,400 Hz  
Confirmation: frequency specification. Delivery: audio file or measurement data + “Audible: Variable”. History: static SKU. Repurchase identical.

23 Unit 12B Unoccupied  
Confirmation: institutional vacancy. Delivery: access credentials or floor-plan extract. History: still unoccupied (or status updated if occupied later).

24 First 12 Sheets  
Confirmation: calibration waste. Delivery: physical sheets + production log. History: “Calibration Discard – Run X”.

**Should Post-Purchase become mandatory?** ONLY FOR SOME PRODUCT TYPES. Mandatory for Temporal Drift, Commerce-Native State, and Condition Collapse. Optional for pure perception or byproduct objects where post-purchase adds little beyond gimmick.

**19. Adversarial Critique**

The generative formula is already legible: almost any bounded ordinary experience can be titled into an OTHER GOODS product. Naming is frequently doing the ontological work that should be done by the collision with actual commerce systems. Many entries remain elegant captions rather than objects that survive cart → payment → fulfillment → history → return. Scaling to 200 SKUs under the current density of soft ontology will produce fatigue and pattern recognition, collapsing the store into a recognizable conceptual-art trick rather than a convincing commercial world. The dryness principle risks becoming a new sentimental mode—cool detachment as aesthetic. Commerce is still under-used as an ontology-producing force rather than a display mechanism.

**Three problems that must be solved before large Catalog**:  
1. Enforce post-purchase depth as a design constraint for at least half the catalog.  
2. Raise the ratio of Commerce-Generated and Commerce-Activated products above 65 %.  
3. Kill or radically rework any title that can be reduced to “the moment / state / absence of X” without a durable commercial collision.

WORKING BUT NEEDS CORRECTION

## Biggest Improvement from v0.4
Introduction of genuinely non-sentimental grammars (frequency threshold, institutional vacancy, process byproduct) and several strong commerce-native objects.

## Biggest Remaining Weakness
Too many products still function primarily as elegant names rather than objects transformed by cart, fulfillment, and order history.

## Strongest Product in v0.5
Upper-Right Corner, Dented in Transit

## Weakest Product in v0.5
6-Minute Unrecorded Conversation

## Best Naming Decision
Residual Warmth on a Park Bench (successful dryness without loss of image)

## Worst Naming Decision
Last 10 Minutes Before Knowing the Ending

## Most Valuable New Grammar Territory
Institutional Absence / Threshold Perception

## Most Dangerous Blind Spot
Post-purchase behavior remains under-designed; the store’s formal power is still concentrated on the product page.

## One Thing to Do Before v0.6
For every retained product, write the complete post-purchase path (confirmation → fulfillment definition → six-month order-history entry → return/repurchase outcome) and discard any product whose path adds only gimmick.

**4. Adversarial Critique**

The primary vulnerability of v0.6 is its creeping desire to intellectualize away raw commercial absurdity. By introducing **Commerce-as-Assertion**, the project risks creating an easy escape hatch for weak, lazy products. If simply asserting that something is a product makes it a product, then *anything* can be listed without the store actually doing work. That is not conceptual rigor; it is just a shop interface wrapped around a book of short prose fragments.

Furthermore, the Relational Framework and System Interventions threaten to turn OTHER GOODS into a self-satisfied curatorial taxonomy. When a store starts selling "Control Over Final Outcome — Unavailable," it stops looking like an e-commerce site calmly selling strange goods and starts looking like an art student's critical design thesis. The viewer stops asking *"What did I just buy?"* and starts thinking *"Ah, this is an allegory about agency."*

The project is also over-correcting against technical bias. `The World I Helped Break` flirts dangerously with cinematic posturing; it sounds like a tagline for a blockbuster movie or a indie game. If OTHER GOODS protects human/existential items simply out of fear of sounding cold, it will pollute its catalog with theatrical sentimentality disguised as "moral agency." A commercial store does not care about your guilt—it cares about liability, terms, and non-refundable deposits.

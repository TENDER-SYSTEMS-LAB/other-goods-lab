# OTHER GOODS — Post-v0.5 → Pre-v0.6 Working Decisions

## Purpose

This document records the working decisions reached through discussion after R&D Collection v0.5 and before composing v0.6.

It is intended as a clean handoff for the OTHER GOODS Wiki and for future v0.6 work.

Important distinction:

- **User-confirmed / jointly-developed directions** are recorded as working decisions.
- **Unresolved items** remain explicitly unresolved.
- External LLM survey results are treated as advisory evidence, not project decisions.

---

# 1. v0.6 Is No Longer a “Better List of Titles”

The main shift from v0.5 is methodological.

v0.5 was still largely evaluated as a set of product concepts and names.

v0.6 should test whether products remain conceptually alive when they are placed inside the full commercial system.

The working objective is:

> **To test what happens when radically different kinds of things are treated as ordinary merchandise.**

The point is not to maximize the number of clever commerce mechanics.

The point is to test different relationships between Product Ontology and Commerce.

---

# 2. Commerce Does Not Need to “Do a Lot” for Every Product

A major correction was made during the discussion.

Earlier evaluation logic tended to assume:

> If Commerce does not materially transform the product, the product is weak.

This is now considered too narrow.

A stronger working test is:

> **Does Commerce create a tension that would not exist without it?**

This allows products where Commerce does almost nothing beyond calmly declaring:

> this is merchandise.

That declaration alone can be conceptually productive.

This is especially important for products such as:

- `Distance to Home: 1,847 mi`
- `Warmth Left on a Park Bench`
- `"These Days," Five Years Ago`
- `The World I Helped Break`

The absence of a complicated post-purchase mechanic is not automatically a weakness.

---

# 3. Three Working Roles for Commerce

These are **experimental observation axes**, not a final Product Grammar taxonomy.

## A. Commerce-Generated

Commerce creates the state or object.

Examples:

- `First Canceled Order`
- `Day After the Return Window Closed`
- `$8.00 Price Drop, 10 Minutes After Purchase`

Without commercial infrastructure, these objects largely do not exist.

## B. Commerce-Transformative

The thing exists independently, but Commerce changes how its identity, condition, ownership, value, or lifecycle is understood.

Examples:

- `Yesterday`
- `First Typo in a New Notebook`
- `Upper-Right Corner, Dented in Transit`
- `17,400 Hz`
- `Unit 12B, Unoccupied`

## C. Commerce-as-Assertion

The thing may already exist and Commerce may barely transform it.

The tension comes from the institution calmly asserting:

> this is a product.

Examples:

- `Distance to Home: 1,847 mi`
- `Warmth Left on a Park Bench`
- `"These Days," Five Years Ago`
- potentially `The World I Helped Break`

This third role is considered an important axis for protecting OTHER GOODS from becoming only a system-mechanics project.

---

# 4. Product Does Not Have to Be the Only Unit of Meaning

A major new decision is that OTHER GOODS should not assume:

> **1 concept = 1 product**

The meaningful unit may instead be:

1. **a singular product**
2. **a relationship between multiple products**
3. **a behavior or intervention of the store itself**

This broadens the work beyond isolated SKU concepts.

---

# 5. Three R&D Unit Types

## 5.1 Singular Product

A single SKU can carry the relevant ontology.

Examples:

- `Yesterday`
- `The World I Helped Break`
- `Distance to Home: 1,847 mi`
- `17,400 Hz`

## 5.2 Relational Product Set

Two or more products must be seen together for the ontology to become visible.

The first important example is the tolerance experiment.

### Tolerance Pair

**0.49 mm Deviation**
- Stock: 1
- Quality Status: Within Tolerance
- For Sale

**0.51 mm Deviation**
- Stock: 1
- Quality Status: Out of Tolerance
- **Not eligible for purchase**

The physical difference is only 0.02 mm.

The commercial difference can be categorical:

- sellable / non-sellable
- pass / fail
- normal inventory / reject inventory
- eligible / ineligible for sale

The ontology is not located in either SKU alone.

It exists in the invisible boundary between them.

The pair should preferably allow the viewer to infer the threshold rather than explicitly explain it.

### Important Commerce Detail

The `Out of Tolerance` product may still have:

- a price,
- inventory,
- a product page,
- specifications,

while remaining impossible to purchase.

This creates a useful separation between:

> **existence**

and

> **sellability**.

A product can exist in stock without being commercially admissible.

---

## 5.3 System-Level Intervention

Some work may belong to the storefront system rather than to a SKU.

Example working direction:

> **Control Over Final Outcome — Unavailable**

This may function better as:

- an unavailable option,
- add-on,
- service,
- warranty,
- checkout choice,
- variant,
- or repeated system behavior,

rather than as a standalone product.

This category remains experimental.

---

# 6. v0.6 Is Not Fixed to Exactly 16 Products

The earlier 12–16 recommendation was useful as a way to force deeper R&D.

It is no longer treated as a hard product-count constraint.

If a genuinely distinct ontology is discovered, v0.6 may grow.

More importantly, the project should distinguish:

> **R&D Units**

from

> **Storefront SKUs**

A relational R&D unit may contain multiple SKUs.

For example:

- 1 Tolerance Pair = 1 R&D unit
- but 2 storefront products

Therefore v0.6 should be counted by conceptual experiments first, not only by SKU count.

---

# 7. Technical-System Bias Is Now an Explicit Risk

The project must actively avoid becoming:

> a collection of technical, administrative, industrial, or system-status products.

Products such as:

- `17,400 Hz`
- `Unit 12B, Unoccupied`
- tolerance states
- printing byproducts
- logistics defects
- refund states

are valuable.

However, they are also easy to explain analytically and easy for LLM rubrics to reward.

If they dominate, OTHER GOODS could drift into:

> a cold conceptual-design project about standards, systems, and institutional classification.

That is not the intended direction.

The Collection must preserve products with human, ordinary, sensory, emotional, temporal, and existential depth.

---

# 8. Product Temperature as a Bias Check

This is **not a final taxonomy**.

It is a simple balancing instrument to prevent one aesthetic/intellectual temperature from dominating the Collection.

## Human / Existential

Examples:

- `Yesterday`
- `"These Days," Five Years Ago`
- `Distance to Home: 1,847 mi`
- `The World I Helped Break`

## Ordinary / Sensory

Examples:

- `Warmth Left on a Park Bench`
- `30th Rain Through the Same Window`
- `12 Stairs, Never Counted`

## Institutional / Material / Technical

Examples:

- `17,400 Hz`
- `Unit 12B, Unoccupied`
- `Tolerance Pair`
- `First 12 Makeready Sheets`

## Commerce-Native

Examples:

- `First Canceled Order`
- `Day After the Return Window Closed`
- `$8.00 Price Drop, 10 Minutes After Purchase`

The goal is **not** equal percentages.

The goal is to ask:

> Is one logic beginning to explain the whole store?

If yes, the Collection is becoming too reducible.

---

# 9. Naming: No Single “Dryness” Rule

The earlier naming direction of maximizing dryness is no longer accepted as a global rule.

Technical or bureaucratic language should not automatically replace philosophical, emotional, or ordinary language.

A product name may carry human meaning.

Commerce can create the collision through:

- price,
- stock,
- eligibility,
- checkout,
- return,
- order history,
- or simply the visual fact that the thing is presented as merchandise.

The naming system should allow register diversity.

A real store may contain:

- specification names,
- plain names,
- status strings,
- ordinary spoken English,
- descriptive titles,
- institutional language.

Uniform bureaucratic cool is itself a stylistic trap.

---

# 10. `The World I Helped Break`

The previous candidate:

> `What Payment Cannot Undo`

was rejected as an adequate expression of the intended source territory.

The problem was that it translated the idea too quickly into payment, debt, and settlement.

The desired source feeling is closer to the structure associated with the discussed “Nolan-type hero”:

- I contributed to the damage.
- I recognize that responsibility.
- guilt expands into responsibility,
- responsibility expands into a wish to control outcomes,
- complete repair becomes impossible,
- action must continue without the fantasy of total control.

The working replacement is:

> **The World I Helped Break**

This title is currently considered much closer to the intended emotional and philosophical source.

It is valuable because the subject is not outside the problem.

The speaker is implicated in the world being sold.

Commerce does not need to explain this through an elaborate mechanic.

The normal product presentation may itself provide enough collision.

Current status:

> **strong v0.6 candidate / working title**

---

# 11. `"These Days," Five Years Ago` Is Restored

This concept was previously dropped too easily.

It should return to the active v0.6 pool.

Its ontology is not identical to `Yesterday`.

## Yesterday

A fixed name points to a referent that moves every day.

## `"These Days," Five Years Ago`

A past moment once experienced itself as the present.

The phrase `"these days"` remains grammatically present-tense even when located five years in the past.

The concept is:

> **the strange fact that the past was once "now."**

This is considered distinct enough from `Yesterday` to test in the same Collection.

The quotation marks may be semantically justified because the phrase itself is the object under examination, not merely decorative punctuation.

Current working title:

> **"These Days," Five Years Ago**

---

# 12. `0.49 mm Deviation` Should Not Stand Alone

The original single product:

> `0.49 mm Deviation, Within Tolerance`

felt too much like an ordinary industrial product specification.

The ontology becomes stronger when presented relationally.

The important subject is not the number itself.

It is:

> **the institutionally created boundary that turns continuous physical variation into categorical commercial reality.**

Therefore the preferred experiment is the **Tolerance Pair**:

- `0.49 mm Deviation` — Within Tolerance / sellable
- `0.51 mm Deviation` — Out of Tolerance / stocked but not sellable

This is one of the strongest current examples of a Relational Product Set.

---

# 13. Lifecycle Testing Remains Important, but Mechanics Are Not Mandatory

Every retained v0.6 R&D unit should be tested through commercial lifecycle questions.

However:

> **Every product must be tested through Commerce.  
> Not every product needs a special Commerce gimmick.**

Useful test surfaces include:

- price
- price logic
- inventory
- sellability
- cart
- purchase
- order confirmation
- fulfillment
- delivery
- order history
- return
- refund
- repurchase
- time

A truthful answer of:

> “nothing conceptually important happens here”

may itself be useful evidence.

The project should not manufacture complexity merely to make the R&D sheet look sophisticated.

---

# 14. Working Transaction Assumption for v0.6

The current working direction is:

> **Product = entry point**  
> **Transaction = activation**

The product remains a meaningful artistic unit.

The transaction may activate, expose, transform, or simply frame that ontology.

For v0.6 R&D, the current working assumption is to simulate normal USD commerce rather than making real-money settlement the focus of the experiment.

This can be revisited later.

# 16. Current v0.6 Design Principle

The strongest current formulation is:

> **A product does not have to explain its ontology alone.**

and:

> **The meaningful unit may be a product, a relationship between products, or a behavior of the store itself.**

At the same time:

> **Commerce does not need to transform everything.  
> It only needs to create a tension that would not exist without the store.**

This is the core methodological change entering v0.6.

---

# 17. Next Step

Compose the first actual v0.6 Collection from this working pool.

Do not optimize for a fixed SKU count.

Optimize for:

- distinct ontology,
- different Commerce roles,
- different emotional/technical temperatures,
- relational possibilities,
- system-level interventions,
- and resistance to a single visible generative formula.

After the first v0.6 draft is composed, collect another independent LLM critique round.

That critique should evaluate not only individual products but also:

- the balance of the Collection,
- the new Commerce-as-Assertion axis,
- relational product sets,
- system-level interventions,
- technical-system bias,
- and whether v0.6 still feels like OTHER GOODS rather than a conceptual systems catalog.

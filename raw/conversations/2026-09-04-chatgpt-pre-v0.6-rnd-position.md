# OTHER GOODS — Pre-v0.6 R&D Position Note

## Purpose

This document records ChatGPT's current position before moving from R&D Collection v0.5 to v0.6.

It is **not a user decision** and should not be promoted into the Wiki as confirmed direction without explicit user approval.

The note synthesizes:

- the current OTHER GOODS Wiki direction,
- four external v0.5 evaluations,
- the discussion around English naming and register,
- and a new product territory inspired by the supplied video analysis of guilt, responsibility, control, atonement, and return-home desire.

The interpretation of Christopher Nolan's work discussed below comes from the supplied video/commentary. It should be treated as the commentator's interpretation, not as an objective statement of authorial intent.

---

# 1. Current Assessment of v0.5

v0.5 is clearly stronger than v0.4.

The largest improvement is that the Collection is no longer dominated by memory, nostalgia, relationships, and quiet personal loss. New products such as:

- `17,400 Hz`
- `Unit 12B, Unoccupied`
- `First 12 Sheets Discarded Before Printing`
- `Upper-Right Corner, Dented in Transit`

opened materially different territories: perception, institutions, industry, logistics, defects, and measurable states.

However, the central weakness has also become clearer.

> The project still risks becoming a catalog of strong titles rather than a complete commerce system that meaningfully changes what those titles are.

The next R&D problem is therefore no longer mainly:

> What other unusual things can become products?

It is increasingly:

> What happens when the strongest products are forced through the entire commercial lifecycle?

---

# 2. Products That Have Earned Deeper Development

Across the v0.5 external evaluations, the following products repeatedly emerged as the strongest candidates:

1. `Yesterday`
2. `First Typo in a New Notebook`
3. `First Canceled Order`
4. `Day After the Return Window Closed`
5. `Upper-Right Corner, Dented in Transit`
6. `$8.00 Price Drop, 10 Minutes After Purchase`
7. `17,400 Hz`
8. `Unit 12B, Unoccupied`
9. `First 12 Sheets Discarded Before Printing`

I no longer think the main question for these products should be KEEP / DROP.

The next question should be whether each one survives:

- pricing,
- inventory,
- purchase,
- order confirmation,
- fulfillment,
- delivery,
- order history,
- return,
- repurchase,
- resale,
- and time.

A strong title that collapses when these systems are applied is not yet a strong OTHER GOODS product.

# 3. Products That Should Move to Bench or Rework

The following products currently look weak enough that they should not occupy core v0.6 slots unless substantially reworked:

- `Wrong-Turn Alley`
- `6-Minute Unrecorded Conversation`
- `Last 10 Minutes Before Knowing the Ending`
- `Before Reading the Reviews`

Several other products remain useful as R&D references but should not automatically advance:

- `Summer, Age 16`
- `One Day of the Trip, No Photos`
- `The Room Before It Became Home`
- `Third Door Opened Today`
- `"These Days," 5 Years Ago`

The important distinction is:

> Emotional content is not the problem by itself.

A warm or sentimental product can remain valuable if Commerce does real conceptual work on it.

The correct rejection rule is not:

> "This is too emotional."

It is closer to:

> "After the name is written, does Commerce still have meaningful work to do?"

---

# 4. Naming Direction: From Dryness to Register Fidelity

The previous naming principle was:

> Use the driest name that preserves the product's essential image.

I now consider that rule too blunt.

It risks replacing sentimental style with another equally repetitive house style: bureaucratic cool.

A better working principle is:

> **Use the native register of the system that would plausibly handle the product. Where no such system exists, use plain natural English.**

Examples:

- audio / measurement → acoustic or specification language
- printing → actual printing-industry terminology
- shipping damage → logistics / claims language
- return policy → commerce policy language
- property / vacancy → real-estate or administrative language
- human experience with no governing system → plain natural English

Dryness should be a consequence of register fidelity, not a goal in itself.

This is especially important for titles such as:

- `Warmth Left on a Park Bench`
- `Residual Warmth on a Park Bench`

The second is colder, but not necessarily more correct.

If the product has no real-world institutional register that would call the phenomenon "residual warmth," plain English may be stronger.

---

# 5. v0.6 Should Be a Deep R&D Set, Not a Larger Collection

My current recommendation is:

> **Do not expand the number of titles yet. Reduce and deepen.**

A useful target would be approximately 12–16 products.

The v0.6 objective should be:

> **Prove that the strongest products survive the full commercial lifecycle.**

For each retained product, the R&D sheet should include at least:

- Customer-facing Name
- Korean Working Meaning
- Product Role
- Commerce Type
- Product Grammar
- Price
- Price Logic
- Inventory
- Inventory Logic
- Order Confirmation
- Fulfillment / Delivery
- Order History after six months
- Return behavior
- Repurchase behavior
- Resale behavior if relevant
- Representation / product image logic
- Failure Test: what disappears if Commerce is removed?

---

# 6. Post-Purchase Behavior

Post-purchase behavior is one of the strongest unresolved areas.

However, I do **not** think elaborate post-purchase mechanics should be mandatory for every product.

A better rule is:

> **If post-purchase behavior reveals conceptual depth that the product page cannot, it must be designed. If it adds only a joke, do not force it.**

It should be especially important for:

- Commerce-Generated products
- Commerce-Activated products
- temporal products
- condition-changing products
- return / refund products
- products whose identity changes over time

Examples:

### Yesterday

The real work may happen six months later in Order History.

### First Canceled Order

The cancellation record may itself be fulfillment.

### Upper-Right Corner Dented in Transit

The return / exchange flow may determine whether the ordered identity is preserved or destroyed.

---

# 7. Price and Inventory Are Still Underdeveloped

Price should no longer be treated as decorative realism.

For every v0.6 product, the system should be able to answer:

> Why does this cost this amount?

Possible logics may include:

- market-like pricing
- fixed nominal pricing
- time-decay pricing
- condition-dependent pricing
- per-unit pricing
- price tied to remaining duration
- deliberately arbitrary retail pricing, if arbitrariness itself is conceptually justified

The same applies to inventory:

> What exactly counts as one unit?

> Can this product sell out?

> Does one customer purchasing it reduce availability for another?

> Does inventory replenish naturally, procedurally, or never?

If the answer is merely "because the product page needs a price and stock field," the product is underdesigned.

---

# 8. The New Moral / Existential Territory

A new direction emerged from the supplied video analysis.

The video's central interpretation is not simply "guilt."

The more useful structure is:

1. A person contributes to irreversible consequences.
2. They recognize their responsibility.
3. Guilt expands into responsibility beyond their actual control.
4. They attempt to control the world in order to prevent repetition.
5. They fail because reality includes forces outside their control.
6. They collapse into helplessness.
7. They eventually accept limited responsibility without abandoning action.

The core conflict is:

> **Responsibility vs. Control**

This suggests a Product Territory that OTHER GOODS has not yet explored deeply:

- moral residue
- responsibility that outlives payment
- consequences that continue after atonement
- responsibility without control
- repair that can only ever be partial
- finite action inside infinite consequences

This territory could reconnect the human depth of early OTHER GOODS with the stronger institutional/systemic language discovered in v0.5.

---

# 9. Why `Account Settled, Consequences Ongoing` Is Not Yet the Right Name

An earlier candidate was:

> `Account Settled, Consequences Ongoing`

The concept is strong, but `Account` narrows the reading too aggressively toward accounting, debt, legal settlement, or bookkeeping.

The underlying idea is larger:

> A person may pay a price, suffer punishment, apologize, repent, or otherwise "pay" for what they did — but the consequences already released into the world do not disappear.

The concept should therefore not be reduced prematurely to an accounting frame.

---

# 10. Preferred Candidate: `What Payment Cannot Undo`

My current preferred candidate is:

> **What Payment Cannot Undo**

Korean working meaning:

> **대가를 치러도 되돌릴 수 없는 것**

Why it is stronger:

- It keeps a Commerce surface through the word `Payment`.
- It does not directly name `guilt`, `atonement`, or `redemption`.
- It does not reduce the idea to legal liability.
- It makes payment itself the limit being tested.
- It can carry human, moral, political, and historical readings without naming any specific source or IP.
- It fits OTHER GOODS's larger question: what happens when Commerce assumes that value, obligation, or completion can be settled through exchange?

Risk:

It is more philosophical and less product-like than names such as `17,400 Hz` or `Unit 12B, Unoccupied`.

That may be acceptable if the commerce system performs enough work after the title.

This product should be tested rather than assumed to be successful.

# 11. Related Experimental Candidates

The same source also suggests several additional R&D directions.

## Self-Assigned Responsibility

Korean working meaning:

> 스스로 떠맡은 책임

Core:

Responsibility that exceeds what was actually assigned or controllable.

Possible system fields:

- Assigned by: Self
- Scope: Expanded
- Control: Limited

Risk:

Too psychological unless the system can formalize the mismatch.

---

## Repair Scope: Partial

Korean working meaning:

> 복구 가능 범위: 일부

Core:

The whole cannot be restored, but some intervention is still possible.

This is potentially important because it avoids both extremes:

- total control
- total helplessness

It represents finite responsibility.

---

## Distance to Home: 1,847 mi

Korean working meaning:

> 집까지 남은 거리 1,847마일

Core:

A concrete, finite destination replacing an impossible obligation to "fix everything."

This can carry longing without naming longing.

Its register may plausibly come from navigation systems rather than poetry.

---

## Control Over Final Outcome — Unavailable

Korean working meaning:

> 최종 결과에 대한 통제권 — 구매 불가

This may work better as a system-level unavailable add-on than as a standalone product.

It directly tests one of OTHER GOODS's core ideas:

> Some things are desirable precisely because they cannot be owned.

---

# 12. Important Warning: Do Not Overcorrect Toward Cold Systems

The v0.5 evaluations correctly identified that perception, institutions, logistics, and industry are valuable new territories.

But following those recommendations too literally could produce a different failure:

> a highly polished conceptual database full of technical states.

OTHER GOODS should not become:

- only sentimental,
- only poetic,
- only bureaucratic,
- only scientific,
- or only meta-commerce.

The most promising direction is not "cold instead of warm."

It is:

> **A normal commercial surface capable of handling objects with radically different emotional, material, institutional, and philosophical temperatures.**

`What Payment Cannot Undo` is interesting precisely because it may connect:

- human guilt and responsibility,
- Commerce's language of payment and settlement,
- irreversible consequences,
- and the limits of exchange.

That bridge is more valuable than simply adding another technical product.

---

# 13. Proposed Pre-v0.6 Questions

Before finalizing v0.6, I would ask external evaluators to challenge the following:

1. Is the move from "dryness" to "register fidelity" correct?
2. Should v0.6 reduce to 12–16 products and deepen lifecycle behavior?
3. Should post-purchase behavior be mandatory only when conceptually generative?
4. Is price now a major missing conceptual surface?
5. Does `What Payment Cannot Undo` belong in OTHER GOODS, or is it too philosophical?
6. Can moral responsibility and atonement become a generative Product Territory without becoming abstract moral philosophy?
7. Does `Repair Scope: Partial` successfully express finite responsibility?
8. Does `Distance to Home: 1,847 mi` preserve emotional depth without reverting to nostalgia?
9. Is `Control Over Final Outcome — Unavailable` stronger as a system option than as a product?
10. Is the project at risk of replacing sentimentality with bureaucracy and taxonomy?

---

# 14. Current Recommendation Before v0.6

Do not finalize v0.6 yet.

First:

1. collect external critique on the current pre-v0.6 position,
2. resolve the naming principle,
3. test the new moral-residue territory,
4. decide whether `What Payment Cannot Undo` belongs in the core set,
5. decide the Product Behavior Sheet fields,
6. then compose v0.6.

The next Collection version should represent a methodological change, not merely another list revision.

---

# Final Position

The strongest insight from v0.5 is that OTHER GOODS is becoming more interesting when Commerce is allowed to do more than display strange objects.

The strongest risk is that this insight could lead to overcorrection: replacing poetic sentimentality with administrative conceptualism.

The best v0.6 should therefore prove two things at once:

> **Commerce must materially change the product.**

and

> **The product must still contain something worth changing.**

# OTHER GOODS

## Summary

OTHER GOODS is an interactive conceptual art project that explores the conceptual collision created when a familiar, highly polished e-commerce system treats things that cannot ordinarily be products as normal merchandise. It does not parody the appearance of a store; it is built with the precision of a real commercial service. Inside it, commerce rules such as price, inventory, search, recommendation, purchase, shipping, ownership, and returns attempt to handle things that cannot be owned or delivered, such as time, sensations, moments, and memories.

## Project Identity

- Name: **OTHER GOODS** (`status: confirmed`, `attribution: user-confirmed`)
- Customer-facing tagline: **A store for other kinds of goods.** (`status: confirmed`, `attribution: user-confirmed`)
- Project statement seed: **Treating the unownable as merchandise.** (`status: confirmed`, `attribution: user-confirmed`)

The language visitors first encounter is distinct from language used to explain the work. Use the former inside the store; develop the latter for contexts such as artist statements and exhibition introductions.

## Central Question

> What happens when a completely normal commerce system treats something that cannot be a product as though it were one?

This question expands into whether something can become a product, whether price changes value, whether purchase and ownership are the same, and whether everything can be compared, shipped, and returned.

## Central Principles

- [[DEC-002-perfect-store-principle]] — Build the store's technical, visual, and usability quality to the level of a real commercial service.
- [[conceptual-contrast]] — The collision between a normal commercial system and noncommercial objects creates the work.
- [[progressive-displacement]] — Once visitors believe they understand a rule, let them discover a different result produced by that same rule.
- [[commerce-as-medium]] — Treat the UI, product data, cart, checkout, and post-order flow as the work's medium.

## Current Scope

Under [[DEC-004-commerce-first]], the first work focuses on **OTHER GOODS / Commerce**. Delivery, Markets, Maps, an anonymous artist persona, a social-media and GitHub world, and blockchain or tokenization remain possible but are deferred from the current implementation scope.

As of 2026-09-04, the Commerce work is in an R&D stage. The Collection operates as an R&D Set for discovering Product Grammar rather than as a final product list ([[DEC-006-collection-before-catalog]]), and product ideas begin with Product Ontology rather than a Commerce function ([[DEC-007-ontology-before-commerce]]). One of two surveys on 2026-09-04 challenged this direction. Because that survey is `llm-proposed`, the decision remains valid, while the unresolved challenge is recorded in [[Q-005-commerce-generative-vs-display]]. The R&D Collection has reached v0.4 with 30 products and has been evaluated by two surveys; see [[v0.4-evaluation]]. See [[rnd-collection]] and [[product-grammar]] for details.

## Still Undecided

- Composition of the first collection and each product's commerce mechanic — partially resolved on 2026-09-04: the current Collection will operate as an R&D Set rather than a final composition ([[DEC-006-collection-before-catalog]])
- Whether real payment is used and what happens after purchase
- Rules for price, inventory, shipping, and returns
- Exhibition format and release scope
- How the artist or OTHER GOODS itself exists
- Whether blockchain and tokenization are necessary
- Final scope of the Product Grammar taxonomy ([[Q-003-product-grammar-taxonomy-scope]])
- Conditions for ending R&D and moving to the Catalog ([[Q-004-rnd-collection-exit-condition]])
- Balance between emotive and dry products
- What to measure in real audience testing
- Whether Commerce amplifies ontology or produces it — [[Q-005-commerce-generative-vs-display]]
- What happens after checkout — [[Q-006-post-purchase-behavior]]. No other Wiki page yet addresses the post-purchase stage.

# Pre-v0.6 R&D Position — Comparison of Six Critiques

## Evidence Status

These six `llm-proposed` responses critique an unadopted pre-v0.6 position; they are not evaluations of an actual v0.6 Collection. The registered ChatGPT position note is the derivative position being critiqued, not a seventh independent evaluation. A separate shared evaluation prompt is not registered. Matching structures, candidates, and forced questions strongly indicate a common brief, but identical prompt delivery and evaluator independence cannot be verified.

Counts therefore represent convergence among six LLM responses, not six user confirmations, audience research, or necessarily independent judgments. The moral-residue material remains downstream of a missing video and attachment; the registered discussion contains only the user's pasted interpretation. Statements about legal or consumer obligations if real money moves are LLM-proposed risk flags, not verified legal guidance.

`READY` in these responses must never be read as project readiness or user approval.

## Position Under Review

The derivative ChatGPT proposal recommends a 12–16-product Deep R&D Set: test selected products through lifecycle pressure, register fidelity, price and inventory logic, and a moral-residue test before composing v0.6. It names five candidates and delays v0.6 composition pending critique and further tests. None of those structures, rules, candidates, or tests is adopted.

## Overall Verdict — 6/6 Identical

All six return `READY WITH ONE REQUIRED CORRECTION`. Their corrections differ: build a transactional slice; make Commerce an agent; define product versus transaction; limit moral-residue entry; reduce the sheet; and require a minimum meaning-changing commerce event.

The only valid synthesis is **directionally ready for another experiment**, not “v0.6 is ready.”

## Shared Conclusions

- 6/6 support reducing and deepening for R&D.
- 6/6 warn that 12–16 engineered products do not supply real catalog density.
- 6/6 say Register Fidelity is stronger than Dryness but can become another house style.
- 6/6 constrain moral-residue entry; none supports unconditional expansion.
- 6/6 identify `Self-Assigned Responsibility` as the weakest candidate.
- 6/6 treat `Control Over Final Outcome — Unavailable` as stronger or more plausible at system level than as an ordinary SKU.
- 6/6 warn that the R&D apparatus or Behavior Sheet can outgrow the artwork.

## Important Splits

- Universal versus selective lifecycle design: 3/3.
- Mandatory price logic: 5/1.
- Mandatory inventory logic: 4/2.
- `What Payment Cannot Undo`: 5 rework, 1 keep.
- The atomic-unit and real-money question remains unresolved.

## Product Behavior Sheet Review

All six treat a customer-facing name, price logic, and a Failure Test as essentials. Inventory logic is essential to five and selective to GLM. Order-history/lifecycle recording is directly retained by five; GLM instead emphasizes default-policy deviations. Only Kimi explicitly retains Product Grammar as essential; the others cut, demote, condition, or omit it. All six reject `Representation Logic` as a compulsory abstract field: use an actual page/mockup or make it optional.

The responses propose, without adopting a final sheet: a unit definition; purchase promise; customer and system action; unresolved remainder; third party; support posture; tax and shipping; and default-policy deviations.

## Methodological Warnings

- No transactional surface has been built.
- No recorded unbriefed human visitor has reached checkout.
- The process risks optimizing for overlapping LLM tastes.
- Push surfaces such as email may reach more purchasers than six-month order-history revisits.
- Deep flagship products and mundane catalog density may require separate tracks.
- Moral-residue work introduces third parties, accusation, appropriation, and tonal risks.

## Additional Candidate Signals

Repeated patterns only are retained here; full generated lists remain in raw. Four of six prefer `Control Over Final Outcome` as the best new direction, with the system-level qualification above. `Minimum Payment` and `What Payment Cannot Undo` appear as single-model alternatives. Generated variants are not active candidates.

## What This Round Does Not Decide

No v0.6 list, item count, lifecycle rule, naming rule, payment model, taxonomy, candidate, or Behavior Sheet is adopted.

# Pre-v0.6 Critique Round 2 — Comparison of Five Surveys

## Evidence Status

These five `llm-proposed` responses critique the working directions recorded in `SRC-2026-09-06-post-v0.5-pre-v0.6-working-decisions`. They are not evaluations of an actual v0.6 Collection; no v0.6 exists. This is the **second** critique round in the pre-v0.6 period and must not be merged with the first, which is compared in [[pre-v0.6-evaluation]] and reviewed a different position note.

`READY WITH MINOR REVISIONS` in these responses must never be read as project readiness, v0.6 approval, or user approval. Counts represent convergence among five responses whose independence is unverified.

### Provenance

The shared evaluation brief behind these five responses is **not registered**, and it is demonstrably not the registered working-decisions document. Its section numbering differs: GLM cites the methodological correction as "Section 3" and the three commerce roles as "Section 4," while the registered document places them at §2 and §3; Qwen's headings show a "1. Project Context" and "2. Why v0.6 Exists" preceding them. Qwen's and DeepSeek's headings also indicate forced questions labelled A–L and a required output section that appear in no registered source. The brief therefore contains material — a project-context section and twelve forced questions — that this repository does not hold.

This is the fifth consecutive evaluation round whose prompt is absent. See `raw/sources.md`.

### A date-ordering caution

All five survey files are dated **2026-09-05**, one day earlier than the working-decisions document dated **2026-09-06**, and all six were registered in the same commit. Their content nonetheless establishes that they are **downstream** of those working decisions: every one of them critiques Commerce-as-Assertion, the Tolerance Pair, the R&D-unit-versus-SKU distinction, `The World I Helped Break`, and the restored `"These Days," Five Years Ago`, none of which exists in any earlier registered source. A filename date does not establish decision order in this repository.

## Position Under Review

The working decisions being critiqued: v0.6 tests products inside the full commercial system rather than producing a better list of titles; Commerce need only create a tension that would not exist without it; three experimental observation axes (Commerce-Generated / Commerce-Transformative / Commerce-as-Assertion); three R&D unit types (singular product / relational product set / system-level intervention); R&D units counted separately from storefront SKUs; no fixed product count; no global dryness rule; and a working transaction assumption of simulated USD commerce. See [[rnd-collection]].

## Overall Verdict — 5/5 Identical

All five return `READY WITH MINOR REVISIONS`. Their required revisions differ: add a feature-localization guardrail and a price doctrine (GLM); rework the moral-residue title into commercial language (Qwen); hold Assertion to a residual-power standard (Grok); enforce institutional friction on Assertion products (Gemini); avoid overcorrecting against technical products (DeepSeek).

The only valid synthesis is **directionally ready for a first composition and a build**, not "v0.6 is ready."

## The One Unanimous Instruction

Question L is the strongest result of the round and the third consecutive round to produce it. All five name the same next step in different words: **build a working store and put it in front of unbriefed non-specialist visitors.** Grok adds the discriminating measure — residual attention *after* the first conceptual recognition fades. Qwen adds the discriminating failure — visitors reading titles "like museum placards" instead of navigating the logistics of buying.

None proposes another critique round. GLM names the loop directly as a structural risk: the project is being steered by LLM critique cycles, "this document included," producing pendulum aesthetics rather than discovered work, and its proposed antidote is that the store itself becomes the instrument — carts, dwell time, support tickets, abandoned checkouts.

### An unexplained reversal, recorded

`Distance to Home: 1,847 mi` received **0 KEEP / 1 REWORK / 3 DROP** (plus two mixed drop-or-rework) from the six critiques in [[pre-v0.6-evaluation]], and Claude selected it as its single forced exclusion. The working-decisions document then places it in the active pool and uses it as a flagship illustration of Commerce-as-Assertion, and all five responses in this round rate it KEEP.

No registered source explains the change. Two readings are available and neither is verified: a `jointly-developed` judgment overrode an `llm-proposed` consensus, which the attribution system permits; or the new Assertion framing supplied a reading the earlier round lacked — GLM's argument here is that the checkout address field *completes* the product and that shipping it home would annihilate it, which no response in the earlier round made. Recorded as an Evolution rather than left as a silent contradiction. See `raw/sources.md`.

## Shared Conclusions

- 5/5 accept relational product sets as first-class R&D units, and 5/5 name the Tolerance Pair the strongest relational unit.
- 5/5 say the unpurchasable SKU should show a price. GLM and Qwen give the same reason in different words: a price states that the cut is about **eligibility, not value**.
- 5/5 treat `Control Over Final Outcome — Unavailable` as a system-level intervention, not a product page. This repeats the 6/6 result of [[pre-v0.6-evaluation]] and is now the most consistently supported structural proposal in the project.
- 5/5 name the separation of R&D units from storefront SKUs, or the relational-set recognition it enables, as the strongest methodological improvement.
- 5/5 name a live unbriefed-visitor store test as the single most important next experiment.
- 5/5 identify the same biggest new risk, in near-identical language: **Commerce-as-Assertion becoming an admission loophole for poetic fragments with price tags.**

## Important Splits

- Commerce-as-Assertion as a settled axis: 3 test only / 2 conditional yes. Nobody rejects it; nobody accepts it unconditionally.
- Capping v0.6 by unit count: 3 no / 2 yes.
- Which product most risks a technical-systems catalog: 3 name the Tolerance Pair — **the same unit 5/5 call the round's strongest structural result.**
- Which product best protects against one: 3 `The World I Helped Break` / 2 `Warmth Left on a Park Bench`.
- Distinctness of `"These Days," Five Years Ago`: 4 yes / 1 no.

## The Proposed Guardrail

GLM supplies the round's only concrete instrument, `llm-proposed` and unadopted: **feature localization.** The tension a product creates must be traceable to one named commercial feature — price, stock, shipping, the address field, condition grade, purchase eligibility, reviews, returns, restock, refund, notification. If the only writable answer is "there is a price tag," the product is a displayed poem rather than merchandise.

Two properties make it worth recording rather than filing with the other proposals. It is **falsifiable**, unlike the reframed question it guards, which GLM argues is unfalsifiable on its own because any strange thing beside a price produces some tension. And it is **not a class penalty**: GLM applies it across temperatures and benches the human product that fails it (`30th Rain`) while keeping assertion-led products that pass through the address field or the condition grade. This bears directly on [[Q-005-commerce-generative-vs-display]], and it sits in Layer 2, so it does not disturb [[DEC-007-ontology-before-commerce]].

## Adversarial Findings

Repeated across three or more responses:

- **The Assertion loophole.** Named by all five as the biggest new risk. Gemini: a shop interface wrapped around a book of prose fragments. Qwen: a gallery with a payment integration.
- **Taxonomy sprawl.** GLM counts the emerging framework at three roles × three unit types × four temperatures = thirty-six cells and warns the curation layer is becoming a second artwork. Grok independently warns the unit-type framework "already risks becoming another taxonomy that the project then has to service." Both add the same constraint: the visitor must never encounter the vocabulary.
- **Didacticism.** Three warn the Tolerance Pair collapses into a diagram once the threshold is noticed. The shared countermeasure is that the refusal stays mute — no tooltip, no tolerance value displayed, no arithmetic performed for the visitor.
- **Temperature as a crutch.** Three warn that balancing by category invites quota logic and keeps weak products for symmetry. GLM reports nearly keeping `30th Rain` for balance and calls that "filler."
- **Technical-product penalty.** DeepSeek and GLM both flag it, and both flag their own fluency bias toward `17,400 Hz` and the Tolerance Pair. DeepSeek's biggest-risk answer inverts the brief's own concern: that fear of coldness produces a collection emotionally balanced and conceptually weaker.
- **The store lacks stakes.** GLM alone: nothing in the pool is scarce, contested, or losable to another customer. Its proposed addition, `The Good Light (West-Facing), 6:40–7:10 PM`, exists to supply rivalry. Single-source.
- **Price is still missing.** GLM again names it the biggest omission of every round so far: in this store price is a philosophical claim, and no round has proposed a pricing doctrine. This repeats its v0.5 finding and remains single-source across both.

## What This Round Does Not Decide

No v0.6 list, unit count, SKU count, candidate, naming rule, price doctrine, lifecycle rule, payment model, guardrail, or taxonomy is adopted. `Commerce-as-Assertion`, the three unit types, and the Tolerance Pair remain `jointly-developed` working directions from the source under review, not confirmed decisions. Unbriefed human evaluation remains absent.

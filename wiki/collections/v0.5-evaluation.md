# R&D Collection v0.5 — Comparison of Four Surveys

## Evidence status

All four responses compared on this page are `llm-proposed`. Where Gemini, GLM, Grok, and Kimi agree, this establishes only that four models agreed with one another; it is not a user decision. This is the same rule applied in [[v0.4-evaluation]] and [[Q-002-first-collection-selection]].

This page carries the same provenance constraint as [[v0.4-evaluation]]. The original “OTHER GOODS — Commerce R&D Collection v0.5” evaluation prompt — the document that contained the 24 candidates, their descriptions, the ten evaluation sections, and the naming principle under test — is **not registered in this repository**. The 24-item list below was reconstructed in reverse from the four surveys' evaluation tables. All four assign identical numbers and identical product titles to all 24 items, which is strong evidence that the reconstruction is accurate; it is nonetheless a reconstruction, not verification against the original text. If the original prompt is obtained, its provenance takes precedence.

Three further items are visible only indirectly through the responses and are recorded as provenance gaps rather than as facts about the prompt:

- The prompt appears to have stated a naming principle for evaluation — *“Use the driest name that preserves the product's essential image”* — because all four surveys quote or paraphrase it under a dedicated section. See [[Q-007-product-naming-register]].
- The prompt appears to have supplied the three-way classification **Commerce-Generated / Commerce-Activated / Commerce-Displayed**, because all four use those exact terms without introducing them. This is the operational form of [[Q-005-commerce-generative-vs-display]].
- Gemini's response refers to visual concepts named **“Machine Blindness”** and **“Placeholder Aesthetics”**, which appear nowhere else in this repository. See [[product-image-system]].

**Carried over (21):** v0.4 01→01, 02→02, 03→03, 04→04, 05→05, 06→06, 07→07, 09→08, 10→09, 11→10, 12→11, 17→12, 18→13, 19→14, 22→15, 25→16, 26→17, 27→18, 28→19, 29→20, 30→21.

**Removed (9):** v0.4 08 A Message Regretted Immediately After Sending, 13 Two People Caught in the Same Rain, 14 That Day for Five People in One Photograph, 15 The Moment a Friend Became a Friend, 16 The Last Time You Did Not Know Was the Last, 20 The Hundredth Greeting to the Same Person, 21 Silence Between Two People, 23 The Second Day of a Trip Not Taken, 24 The Next Conversation After an Unsent Reply.

**Added (3):** 22 `17,400 Hz`, 23 `Unit 12B, Unoccupied`, 24 `First 12 Sheets Discarded Before Printing`.

Two observations follow from this reconstruction and are the most important structural facts about v0.5.

1. **The v0.4 surveys' Bottom recommendations were acted on.** All four products both v0.4 surveys placed in their Bottom 8 (08, 15, 21, 24) are gone, and five further items on Claude's nostalgia-risk list were removed with them.
2. **The three new products are direct instantiations of Claude's v0.4 proposed Grammars.** Claude's `Below Threshold` example was “a refrigerator sound at 17,400 Hz”; its `Institutionally Real` example was “unoccupied 12B”; its `Byproduct` example was “the first 12 sheets discarded before printing.” v0.5 adopted all three examples as products almost verbatim. This means the strongest-rated new territory in v0.5 originates in an `llm-proposed` suggestion, not in a user-originated idea — a single-source dependency worth watching.

## Verdict — 4/4 identical

All four surveys returned **WORKING BUT NEEDS CORRECTION**, the same verdict both v0.4 surveys returned. Two consecutive rounds have now produced the same non-verdict, which is itself a signal for [[Q-004-rnd-collection-exit-condition]]: the Collection is not converging toward a “ready” assessment by adding and swapping candidates.

Their diagnoses have nonetheless shifted in a common direction. In v0.4 the correction demanded was about *which products exist*. In v0.5 all four locate the correction in *what the store does after the product page*. GLM states it most directly: “The needed correction has shifted from what the products are to what the store does.”

## Agreement — Top

Six products appear in all four Top 8 lists: **02 Yesterday, 04 First Typo in a New Notebook, 05 First Canceled Order, 08 Upper-Right Corner, Dented in Transit, 22 17,400 Hz, 23 Unit 12B, Unoccupied**.

Two more appear in three of four: **07 Day After the Return Window Closed** (all but Kimi) and **24 First 12 Sheets Discarded Before Printing** (all but GLM, which calls it a near-miss pending a name and ontology fix).

Appearing once only: 15 Residual Warmth (GLM), 09 $8.00 Price Drop (Kimi).

This is a markedly stronger consensus than v0.4, where only five of thirty were shared between two surveys. Note what the agreed Top contains: three commerce-native or commerce-transformed items (05, 07, 08), two of the three brand-new territories (22, 23), and the two oldest survivors (02, 04).

## Agreement — Bottom

**11 Wrong-Turn Alley** is the only product placed in the Bottom by all four.

Three of four: **13 6-Minute Unrecorded Conversation** (all but Gemini) and **01 Summer, Age 16** (all but GLM).

Two of four: 03 Last 10 Minutes Before Knowing the Ending, 06 Before Reading the Reviews, 12 One Day of the Trip, No Photos, 16 Third Door Opened Today.

## Direct conflicts

Unlike v0.4, where the conflict ran along a single axis, v0.5's disagreements are scattered. They are recorded here without resolution.

- **01 Summer, Age 16.** Gemini, Grok, and Kimi place it at or near the bottom (“nostalgia branding,” “commerce adds nothing”). GLM keeps it and rates its concept 4/5, arguing that one-SKU/many-private-referents is a top-tier grammar and only the chosen referent is clichéd — fixable if customer reviews are built to instantiate it, which would move it from Displayed to Activated.
- **14 30th Rain and 17 12 Stairs (the counting family).** Gemini drops both as “arbitrary” and “filler.” Grok, GLM, and Kimi all keep both, and Grok and GLM value them specifically as the counterweight to the collection's first/last obsession. GLM adds that 17 is the only customer-*verifiable* product, so review disputes about the count become a real mechanic.
- **15 Residual Warmth on a Park Bench — naming.** Grok and Kimi name this v0.5's **best** naming decision; GLM names it the **worst** and asks to revert to “Warmth Left on a Park Bench” or “Warm Spot on a Park Bench.” Same string, opposite verdicts. See [[Q-007-product-naming-register]].
- **16 Third Door Opened Today.** Kimi keeps it (“excellent counting the uncounted”) and lists its name among the strong ones; GLM and Grok call it redundant whimsy whose grammar survives better in 14 and 17.
- **19 The Room Before It Became Home.** Gemini and Kimi drop it, Kimi naming it the worst naming decision (“real-estate poem”). GLM keeps it with the collection's highest residue score and calls it the essential human anchor for the final catalog.
- **21 “These Days,” 5 Years Ago.** GLM drops it as a weaker duplicate of Yesterday and names it the weakest product in v0.5; Grok keeps it as a “solid longer-scale complement to Yesterday.”

## Agreement — the generative formula is visible

This is the sharpest 4/4 agreement in the round, and it is a criticism of the method rather than of any product.

- Gemini: the project “mistakes clever copywriting for ontological friction”; a price tag beside a phrase does not alter the concept.
- Grok: “almost any bounded ordinary experience can be titled into an OTHER GOODS product”; naming is doing the work that collision with commerce should do.
- GLM: “[Article/ordinal] + [ordinary noun] + [condition qualifier]” produces a plausible title for anything, and **the grammar has no rejection criterion**. Its proposed falsifiable test: *does a commerce mechanism — price logic, policy, inventory behavior, review semantics, delivery — do conceptual work that changes what the thing means?*
- Kimi: “a Mad Libs for melancholy and banality”; once a visitor sees 16 and 17 they can predict “Fourth Coffee of the Morning.”

GLM's rejection criterion is the only concrete instrument proposed. It is `llm-proposed` and unadopted, and it would, if applied, cut against [[DEC-007-ontology-before-commerce]] — see [[Q-005-commerce-generative-vs-display]].

## Agreement — the second bias

All four warn that v0.5 has not cured the sentimental bias so much as begun to add a second one beside it.

- Kimi (as its most dangerous blind spot): the project risks moving from “a shop that sells memories” to “a shop that sells conceptual categories.” Both are equally reducible.
- GLM: three v0.5 titles already use the “Noun, Status” template; at 200 SKUs that becomes “the sentimentality of bureaucratic cool.” It adds that the correction “added cold objects rather than discriminating among warm ones,” and that v0.4's weak products failed because commerce had nothing to do, not because they were tender.
- Gemini: if everything is named “Residual X” or “Temporal State Y,” the store devolves into predictable bureaucratic parody.
- Grok: pure dryness becomes its own aesthetic formula.

GLM raises a related risk specific to [[progressive-displacement]]: warm/cold alternation can itself become the displacement formula.

## Commerce-Generated / Activated / Displayed

All four applied the three-way classification. Their counts:

| Survey | Generated | Activated | Displayed | Recommended target |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Gemini | not counted | not counted | “heavily skewed” to Displayed | 40 / 40 / 20 |
| Grok | 3 (12%) | 9 (40%) | 12 (48%) | 20–25 / 45–50 / ≤30 |
| GLM | 4 (17%) | 12 (50%) | 8 (33%) | ~25 / ~50 / 20–25 |
| Kimi | 3 (12.5%) | 12 (50%) | 9 (37.5%) | 20–25 / 50–60 / 15–25 |

**4/4 judge the current balance unhealthy, with too large a Displayed share.** Three of four converge on a target of roughly 20–25% Generated; Gemini alone asks for 40%.

The classification is not yet operationally stable, and this matters more than the counts. Gemini lists **Yesterday, 17,400 Hz, and Unit 12B as Commerce-Displayed** products worth keeping; GLM classifies all three as **Commerce-Activated**; Kimi lists Yesterday under Activated in its table and then again under “Displayed products worth keeping.” The same three products are assigned to different categories by different evaluators, so the ratios above are not directly comparable. Recorded in [[Q-005-commerce-generative-vs-display]].

GLM additionally argues the dichotomy behind the axis is false: “Commerce-generated products *are* ontology-first products whose ontology happens to be commercial,” and that the instruction should be to mine commerce's own ontologies *and* collide commerce with non-commercial ones.

## Post-purchase — should the field be mandatory?

All four ran the post-purchase test on their Top 8, and all four name the post-purchase layer as the decisive next step. They split on whether the field should be mandatory.

- **YES** — Gemini (“the fulfillment logic is the actual art piece”) and GLM, with an important qualification: mandatory as a *test*, not as a *feature*. If the honest answer is “nothing meaningful happens after payment,” that is diagnostic — the product is Commerce-Displayed and the catalog must ration those.
- **ONLY FOR SOME PRODUCT TYPES** — Grok (mandatory for Temporal Drift, Commerce-Native State, Condition Collapse; optional elsewhere) and Kimi (mandatory for Generated and Activated; forcing it on Displayed products creates gimmicks). Kimi's test: *does post-purchase behavior reveal something the product page could not?*

This resolves the single-source dependency previously noted in [[Q-006-post-purchase-behavior]], where only Claude's v0.4 survey addressed the topic. Four independent models now agree the area is decisive; they do not agree it should be a universal gate.

## Grammar consolidation — four proposals

Each survey proposed a consolidated taxonomy. Against the 25 categories criticized in v0.4, all four land far lower.

- **Gemini — 5:** Commercial Byproducts, Systematic Voids, Indexical Shifters, Perceptual Thresholds, State Irreversibility.
- **Grok — 8:** Temporal Drift, Condition Collapse, Commerce-Native State, Institutional Absence, Threshold Perception, Process Byproduct, Artificial Unit, Defect as Identity.
- **Kimi — 9:** Referential Instability, Condition as Identity, Commerce-Native States, Latent Quantification, Perceptual Thresholds, Institutional Ghosts, Temporal Commodification, Production Byproducts, Trace Phenomena.
- **GLM — 11:** Unstable Referent, Unrecoverable Condition, Transaction Artifact, Unrecorded Existence, Untracked Count, Viewpoint Product, Instrument-Only Object, Institutional Phantom, Process Byproduct, Decaying Trace, Threshold State.

Five categories appear in **all four** proposals under different names. `attribution: llm-synthesis` — the grouping below is this Wiki's reading, not a claim made by any survey.

| Converged category | Gemini | Grok | GLM | Kimi |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Referent drifts under a fixed name | Indexical Shifters | Temporal Drift | Unstable Referent | Referential Instability |
| Condition/irreversibility as identity | State Irreversibility | Condition Collapse | Unrecoverable Condition | Condition as Identity |
| Measurable but imperceptible | Perceptual Thresholds | Threshold Perception | Instrument-Only Object | Perceptual Thresholds |
| Byproduct of making something else | Commercial Byproducts | Process Byproduct | Process Byproduct | Production Byproducts |
| Administratively real, physically absent | Systematic Voids | Institutional Absence | Institutional Phantom | Institutional Ghosts |

A sixth, **commerce events as objects**, is explicit in three (Grok's Commerce-Native State, GLM's Transaction Artifact, Kimi's Commerce-Native States); Gemini folds it into Systematic Voids alongside institutional absence.

Two points of note. First, GLM observes that `3 Seconds of Ocean` is not a grammar at all but names the store's **universal operation** — unitization of the unownable — and functions as the collection's meta-product. Second, Claude's v0.4 proposal to **demote Irreversible from top-level status** was not followed by any of the four: every v0.5 taxonomy keeps a condition/irreversibility grammar at top level.

All four proposals are `llm-proposed` and unadopted. See [[Q-003-product-grammar-taxonomy-scope]].

## Missing territories proposed

All four proposed new territories, and they overlap heavily: logistics limbo and shipping states (Gemini, GLM, Kimi), infrastructure and maintenance (all four), legal and bureaucratic fictions (Gemini, GLM), standards and tolerance thresholds (Gemini, Grok, Kimi), machine and idle states (Gemini, Grok, GLM, Kimi), metrology (GLM's *The Retired Kilogram* — the physical artifact that defined the kilogram until 2019, physically unchanged and ontologically retired).

GLM names the pattern worth carrying forward: most of these already have real commercial or administrative language attached — claim categories, service statuses, lease terms — and “the store is most convincing where a real market already handles adjacent objects.” This is the same observation that drives its naming principle in [[Q-007-product-naming-register]].

## Displacement sequences

Each survey proposed its own 10-step [[progressive-displacement]] sequence. No two sequences agree on the opening product: Gemini opens with 04 First Typo, Grok with 15 Residual Warmth, GLM with 19 The Room Before It Became Home, Kimi with 16 Third Door Opened Today. Three of four end on a commerce-native or ordinary product rather than a climax — GLM ends with 17 12 Stairs specifically so that “the store exits into the visitor's building.”

The disagreement about the opening is not trivial, because Claude's v0.4 observation that the audience fixes the genre from the first three or four items makes the opening the load-bearing choice. Four models given the same 24 products chose four different openings.

## Final verdict fields

| Field | Gemini | Grok | GLM | Kimi |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Strongest product | 05 First Canceled Order | 08 Upper-Right Corner | 02 Yesterday | 02 Yesterday |
| Weakest product | 01 Summer, Age 16 | 13 6-Minute Conversation | 21 “These Days,” 5 Years Ago | 06 Before Reading the Reviews |
| Best naming | 07 Day After the Return Window Closed | 15 Residual Warmth | 22 17,400 Hz | 15 Residual Warmth |
| Worst naming | 12 One Day of the Trip, No Photos | 03 Last 10 Minutes… | 15 “Residual Warmth” | 19 The Room Before It Became Home |
| Most valuable new territory | Commercial / Industrial Byproducts | Institutional Absence / Threshold Perception | Instrument-Only Objects | Perceptual Thresholds |
| Most dangerous blind spot | Visual output system + post-purchase logistics | Post-purchase under-designed | **Price**, never examined as a conceptual surface | Believing that reducing sentimentality is enough |
| One thing before v0.6 | Design the full post-purchase flow for exactly one abstract product | Write the complete post-purchase path for every retained product; discard gimmick-only ones | Wire the complete transaction loop — real prices, confirmation, six-month order history, working returns — for the eight strongest | Cut Displayed products failing the post-purchase test; add ≥6 Generated/Activated; do not exceed 30 until Displayed < 20% |

Three of four name the perception/threshold territory as the most valuable; Gemini names byproducts. On ranking the four new-territory products, Grok, GLM, and Kimi all rank `17,400 Hz` first, while Gemini ranks `First 12 Sheets` first and `17,400 Hz` third.

**4/4 converge on the “one thing before v0.6”: build the transaction and post-purchase layer, not more product names.** They differ only in scope — one product (Gemini), the top eight (GLM), or all retained products (Grok, Kimi).

GLM's blind spot is unique and unsupported by any other survey: **price is treated everywhere as a display field and nowhere as a conceptual surface**, yet it is the store's most confident commercial signal and the first place arbitrary thinking will show at catalog scale. Single-source; recorded here and in [[current-state]] as an open question without its own page.

GLM also raises a meta-risk worth recording verbatim in substance: the project's evaluation apparatus — versioned taxonomies, grammar consolidation, external critique rounds — can become the real artwork, with the store as its illustration. Visitors will only ever meet the storefront.

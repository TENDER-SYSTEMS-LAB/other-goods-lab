# R&D Collection v0.4 — Comparison of Two Surveys

## Evidence status

Both responses compared on this page are `llm-proposed`. Even where Gemini and Claude reach the same assessment (for example, agreed Top, agreed Bottom, or the criticism of nostalgia bias), this establishes only that the two models agreed with each other; it is not a user decision. This is the same rule [[Q-002-first-collection-selection]] applies to its comparison of the three v0.1 surveys.

This page also has a provenance constraint absent from v0.2/v0.3. The original “OTHER GOODS — Commerce R&D Collection v0.4 Evaluation Prompt”—the document that would have contained the list of 30 candidates and evaluation criteria—is not registered in this repo. The list of 30 candidates included here was reconstructed in reverse from the surveys' evaluation tables. The fact that both surveys assign entirely identical numbers and product titles to all 30 is strong evidence that this reconstruction is accurate; nonetheless, it is a reconstruction, not confirmation against the original text. If the original prompt is separately obtained, its provenance takes precedence.

## Agreement — Top

The five products both surveys placed in their Top 8, along with their respective reasons (Gemini / Claude):

- **02 Yesterday** — Gemini: “It dramatically collides the property of time, whose referent changes every day, with the rigidity of SKU and inventory systems.” / Claude: “As time passes, the product title turns false on its own, and the commerce apparatus of order history preserves that falsehood.”
- **04 The First Typo in a New Notebook** — Gemini: “It brings irreversible state change into the standards of ‘used’ and ‘new product,’ presenting a clear logic.” / Claude: “A case physically identical yet changed only in condition grade. It is not sentimental, and it quietly collapses the logic of resale.”
- **09 The Upper-Right Corner Dented During Shipping** — Gemini: “The defect itself becomes the product's identity, perfectly overturning the concept of a ‘new product’ and the shipping system.” / Claude: “A structure in which a request for exchange destroys the ordered item. It has the highest logical density of collision among the 30.”
- **22 Warmth Left on a Park Bench** — Gemini: “It excellently contrasts a sensory, temporary state with ownership, the basic concept of commerce.” / Claude: “The sole non-commerce product that begins with matter rather than memory. It naturally calls for a new mechanic: an expiration limit.”
- **28 The Room Before Calling It “Home”** — Gemini: “It exceptionally captures how categorization and naming assign meaning.” / Claude: “The proposition that naming changes an object explains the operating principle of the entire Collection.”

Only Gemini placed the following in its Top 8: 17 One Day of a Trip Without Photographs, 25 The Third Door Opened Today, 11 The Reverse Side of a Building Visible Only from a Bus Window.
Only Claude placed the following in its Top 8: 05 The First Canceled Order, 07 The Day After the Refund Period Ends, 10 A ₩8,000 Price Drop Ten Minutes After Purchase.

## Agreement — Bottom

The four products both surveys placed in their Bottom 8: **08 A Message Regretted Immediately After Sending, 15 The Moment a Friend Became a Friend, 21 Silence Between Two People, 24 The Next Conversation After an Unsent Reply**. Both surveys reached the same diagnosis—that they are sentimental memes/SNS captions and that the material itself has already been exhausted.

Only Gemini placed the following in its Bottom 8: 05 The First Canceled Order, 07 The Day After the Refund Period Ends, 10 A ₩8,000 Price Drop Ten Minutes After Purchase, 13 Two People Caught in the Same Rain.
Only Claude placed the following in its Bottom 8: 16 The Last Time You Did Not Know Was the Last, 01 Summer at Sixteen, 14 That Day for Five People in One Photograph, 20 The Hundredth Greeting to the Same Person.

## Direct Conflict — 05 / 07 / 10

This is the most important section on this page. **Gemini placed 05, 07, and 10 in its Bottom 8, while Claude placed the same three products in its Top 8.** Their assessments are completely opposed.

- Gemini's rationale: 05 is “too immersed in Commerce functionality and may look like an e-commerce gag”; 10 is “an extension of a simple consumer complaint rather than conceptual art”; and 07, like 05 and 10, “depends too much on Commerce functionality rather than on a property of the object itself.” Gemini considers commerce-nativeness itself a weakness.
- Claude's rationale: 05 makes “the audience not observers but producers—the Collection's greatest performativity”; 07 “sells not an experience but an extinguished right—a type of object absent from the taxonomy”; and 10 makes “price itself the object—self-reference in that this product's price can also go down.” Claude considers Commerce's generation of objects the Collection's greatest strength.

This opposition lies precisely on the axis decided in [[DEC-007-ontology-before-commerce]]. That decision places Product Ontology (Layer 1) ahead of Commerce Collision (Layer 2), whereas Claude's assessment of 05, 07, and 10 effectively directly rebuts that decision—Claude proposes the alternative formulation that “Commerce is not an amplifier but an institution that produces ontology.” This issue is addressed in [[Q-005-commerce-generative-vs-display]]. No conclusion is reached here; the conflict is recorded accurately.

## Agreement — Greatest Risk

Both surveys identified nostalgia/sentimental bias as the Collection's greatest risk.

- Gemini: “A tendency to concentrate on nostalgia or sentimental narratives such as time, memory, and relationships.” It identifies 01, 08, 13, 15, and 24 as risky products and notes a risk of seeming like a “Cyworld-sentiment shop.”
- Claude: In the General Audience Test, Type 2 (“Ah, so these are this kind of sentimental products”) accounts for 13 items, 43%. It identifies 01, 08, 13, 14, 15, 16, 17, 19, 20, 21, and 24 as risky products. It additionally observes that “the problem is not the proportion but the order of contact”—the audience fixes the genre from the first three or four items in the product list.

Products identified by both surveys: 01, 08, 13, 15, 24. This bias issue directly connects to [[Q-001-comprehensibility-vs-mystery]].

## Grammar Consolidation Proposals

Both surveys judged the current 25-Grammar taxonomy to be over-segmented.

Shared proposals: absorb Scale-distorted into Unitization. Integrate the Shared-but-different family with other Grammars.

- Gemini: DROP Counterfactual / Branching Possibility (as it generally ends in a narrative of regret). Merge Shared-but-different into Unassignable. Merge Irreversible / Negative Value into Irreversible State Change.
- Claude: Combine Non-standardizable + Shared-but-different + Perspective into “One SKU / Many Referents.” Absorb Accumulation into Repeatable-but-not-identical. Absorb Viewpoint-dependent into Location-bound. Absorb Unlived Possibility into Counterfactual. **Demote Irreversible from its top-level Grammar status and reclassify it as a common background property of other Grammars.** This would reduce 25 categories to 11–12, at which point, Claude argues, the taxonomy would finally become a research tool. Claude also criticizes the current 25 as “closer to labels retrofitted to 30 names than discovered classifications.”

The point of divergence: Gemini recommends discarding Counterfactual itself, whereas Claude recommends retaining Counterfactual (because the combination of infinite inventory, zero cost, and unavailable reviews is distinctive) but changing its instances.

Both proposals are `llm-proposed` and remain unadopted. See [[Q-003-product-grammar-taxonomy-scope]].

## Proposed New Grammars

**None of the proposals below has been adopted.**

Gemini's five:
- Formless Material — Material that does not retain a fixed form. Example: 100 ml of fog in a sealed container.
- Perceptual Threshold — A stimulus at the threshold of perception for which it is difficult to prove whether it exists. Example: the volume level at which sound begins to become inaudible.
- Symbiotic / Dependent — A parasitic product that retains its property only when another specific object is present. Example: a vibration sound that occurs only through a specific brand of speaker.
- Absolute Void — A piece of time and space in which no event occurred in the first place. Example: four minutes at an intersection where nothing happened.
- Scale-incomprehensible — A scale that cannot be grasped in ordinary human units. Example: the time it takes for a tectonic plate to move 1 mm.

Claude's six:
- Maintained — An object whose existence is maintained only through someone's continuing labor. Example: the white lines of an athletic field redrawn every morning.
- Institutionally Real — An object without physical substance that is real by an institution's declaration. Example: unoccupied 12B.
- Byproduct — Something produced as a side effect of an intended production. Example: the first 12 sheets discarded before printing begins.
- Below Threshold — A difference that can be measured but cannot be perceived by people. Example: a refrigerator sound at 17,400 Hz that people cannot hear.
- Non-fungible by Use — Something substitutable by specification but made non-substitutable by its history of use. Example: a cup replaced with the same model.
- Perishable Immaterial — An immaterial thing that continues to disappear after purchase. Example: warmth that has cooled by half during shipping.

Notable convergence: **Gemini's Perceptual Threshold and Claude's Below Threshold were proposed independently, yet are nearly the same concept.** The fact that the two models identified the same gap without seeing each other's responses makes this a relatively strong signal.

Shared direction: both recommend moving away from memory, time, and first-person experience toward material, perception, institutions, and industry.

## Claude-Only Observations

These observations appear in Claude's response but not Gemini's.

1. **A split revealed by the document structure itself** — Items 01–10 have a `Primary Commerce Collision` field, while items 11–30 do not. Claude reads this not as an editorial omission but as a diagnosis: the latter 20 were made without considering Commerce in the first place.
2. **The space after payment is empty** — All 30 are designed only through the product-page stage. No e-commerce-specific post-purchase approach—such as an order-confirmation email, shipment tracking, order history, or a repurchase reminder six months later—has been written. Claude identifies this as the most dangerous blind spot and the single thing that must change before v0.5. See [[Q-006-post-purchase-behavior]].
3. **A single emotional temperature** — Claude argues that all 30 are written with the same emotional temperature (quiet loss), making [[progressive-displacement]] physically unable to operate. Displacement occurs not through grammatical variation but only through emotional betrayal.
4. **Exposure of the generative rule** — Claude criticizes the product titles as infinitely generable in the form `[ordinary experience] + [conditional modifier] + [quantity or moment]`, and calls it self-contradictory to describe something infinitely generable as “something that cannot be owned.”
5. **A proposed rule for distinguishing product titles** — If a product title contains two or more conditional relative clauses, it should be considered to lean toward poetry. 02 (Yesterday) and 25 (The Third Door Opened Today) pass, while 16, 24, and 12 are caught by this rule.

## Product-Title Criticism

Both surveys address product-title problems in a separate section. Shared observations: **06**—the concept (Observation-dependent) is A-grade, but the title blocks entry; **27**—the title itself is already poetic rather than the concept of unit division (Unitization).

Claude additionally proposes retitlings for 03, 07, 12, 19, and 28.

Claude's “Weak concept disguised by strong name” category: 27, 21, 13, 15, 08, 24, 01—cases where the sentence is attractive but the ontology beneath it is exhausted by a single sentence.

16 is a special case. Its Grammar (retrospective attribution of identity) ranks highly, but the product title has already completed that ontology; in practice, it functions like a third category, “a weak concept concealed by a strong name.” Claude's assessment is that this is the most dangerous combination: a good Grammar prematurely discarded by a bad title.

## Final Verdict Comparison

Both surveys agree on the final assessment: **WORKING BUT NEEDS CORRECTION**. Their prescriptions, however, are opposed.

- Gemini: Increase the share of physical and action-based products that exclude sentiment. Remove narrative-centered Grammars such as Counterfactual and Boundaryless.
- Claude: Increase the commerce-generative share to 40–50%. Make a `Post-Purchase Behavior` field mandatory for every product, remove products that cannot fill that field, and reduce the 30 to 12.

Their common ground is that “the sentimental bias must be broken”; their difference is what should fill the resulting gap—Gemini says material and perception, while Claude says Commerce mechanisms.

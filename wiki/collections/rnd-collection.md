# R&D Collection

## What This Is

> The current Collection is not a final product list, but an R&D Set for discovering OTHER GOODS's Product Grammar.

This is the current decision reached after discussion of Collection scale and Catalog scale. A Catalog (100–200 SKUs) is needed to create the density of a real e-commerce service—search, categories, recommendations, pagination, sold-out items, and a long tail—but it will not be built now. The user prefers to first make the Product Grammar concrete and more sophisticated through the Collection, then expand to a Catalog. When the Collection will finish this R&D role and move to Catalog expansion remains undecided. See [[DEC-006-collection-before-catalog]] for this sequencing decision and [[Q-004-rnd-collection-exit-condition]] for the exit condition.

## Evaluation Framework

Product candidates were evaluated along two types of axes: structural evaluation and audience response.

Structural evaluation axes:
- **Intrinsic Strength** — How strong is the product title/concept itself when the shopping-mall format is removed?
- **Commerce Dependency** — How greatly does its meaning change when it enters a shopping system?
- **Accessibility** — How quickly can a general audience imagine or respond to it without an artwork explanation?
- **Freshness** — Does it avoid feeling like an idea already familiar from sentimental brands, advertising, contemporary art, internet memes, and so on?
- **Commerce Collision** — How richly does it collide with Price, Inventory, Search, Recommendation, Comparison, Rating, Review, Cart, Purchase, Payment, Shipping, Tracking, Ownership, Condition, Return, Refund, Resale, Sold Out, and so on?

Audience response axes:
- **Hook** — Does it make someone stop or click in a product list?
- **Personal Freshness** — Does it feel personally new to the audience?
- **Residue** — Does a question or image remain after seeing it?
- **Desire Type** — One of WANT / CURIOSITY / IMPOSSIBLE WANT / WHY? / AVOID / INDIFFERENT / REMEMBER.

The important conclusion is that the Collection should not contain only products with high average scores. Products with distinct roles—such as Broad Hook, Conceptual Anchor, Polarizer, System Piece, and Dry Counterweight—must be mixed together. The rhythm created by that mix is the audience experience described by [[progressive-displacement]].

## Criticism of v0.3 — Rejected Framework

`status: rejected`

The user identified an important problem with v0.3.

> The overall Product Grammar is too e-commerce-like. It focuses too heavily on “products whose meaning grows when they meet e-commerce data,” and needs a broader Product Grammar beyond that.

Before this criticism, the framework treated Commerce functions themselves as Product Grammar, using categories such as Return Grammar, Review Grammar, Cart Grammar, and Shipping Grammar. This framework was rejected because it risked narrowing OTHER GOODS too much into “a meta-work that dismantles the e-commerce system.” It was subsequently revised to separate Product Ontology (Layer 1) from Commerce Collision (Layer 2). See [[DEC-007-ontology-before-commerce]] for details.

## v0.4 Plan and Actual Outcome

The following was a plan originally recorded as an as-yet-unexecuted `llm-proposed` proposal.

1. Retain 8–10 strong commerce-native candidates from the existing R&D v0.3.
2. Create 15–20 new candidates that begin with Product Ontology.
3. Compose an R&D Collection v0.4 of approximately 25–30 items.
4. Record the following 10 fields for each product: Product Grammar, Primary Commerce Collision, Secondary Commerce Collision, Intrinsic Strength, Commerce Dependency, Accessibility, Freshness, Residue, Main Risk, Collection Role.
5. Develop 8–10 representative products further to the level of actual Product Pages.
6. Gradually stabilize OTHER GOODS Product Grammar through that process.
7. Begin expansion to a Catalog of 100–200 SKUs once Product Grammar is sufficiently stable.

**(2026-09-04)** v0.4 was actually made and evaluated in two surveys (Gemini and Claude). However, the per-product record of 10 fields and the further development of 8–10 representative products to Product Page level anticipated by the plan cannot be confirmed—the surveys evaluate product titles and concepts only.

## v0.5 (24 items)

**(2026-09-04)** v0.5 was composed of 24 items and evaluated in a third survey round by four models: Gemini, GLM, Grok, and Kimi. As with v0.4, **the original prompt is not in this repository**, and the list was reconstructed from the four surveys' evaluation tables; all four assign identical numbers and titles to all 24. The full list, the evaluations, and the reconstruction of what changed from v0.4 appear in [[v0.5-evaluation]] and are not repeated here.

This is the first version to **shrink**: 30 → 24. Nine v0.4 candidates were removed — including all four that both v0.4 surveys had placed in their Bottom 8 — and three were added.

Two formal changes arrived with v0.5 and are not explained by any registered source: the product titles are now **English** rather than translated Korean, and the price unit changed from **₩ to $** (`A ₩8,000 Price Drop` became `$8.00 Price Drop, 10 Minutes After Purchase`). Neither is recorded anywhere as a user decision. The naming question this raises is tracked in [[Q-007-product-naming-register]].

The three added products — `17,400 Hz`, `Unit 12B, Unoccupied`, and `First 12 Sheets Discarded Before Printing` — are near-verbatim instantiations of the example objects Claude proposed under its new Grammars in the v0.4 survey (`Below Threshold`, `Institutionally Real`, `Byproduct`). All four v0.5 surveys rate this new territory as the collection's most valuable. That makes the strongest-rated material in v0.5 dependent on a single `llm-proposed` source, which is a single-source dependency worth watching rather than a confirmed direction.

### What the third round changed in the diagnosis

All four surveys returned **WORKING BUT NEEDS CORRECTION**, the same verdict as both v0.4 surveys. Two consecutive rounds ending in the same non-verdict is itself evidence for [[Q-004-rnd-collection-exit-condition]]: adding and swapping candidates is not moving the Collection toward a “ready” assessment.

What did change is where the surveys locate the problem. In v0.4 the correction demanded concerned *which products exist*; in v0.5 all four locate it in *what the store does after the product page*. All four name the transaction and post-purchase layer as the single thing to build before v0.6, and all four independently observe that the collection's generative formula — an ordinary phenomenon plus a condition or count — is now visible and has no rejection criterion. Details in [[v0.5-evaluation]].

## Why Product Pages Do Not Yet Exist

Of the 26 candidates, only two—“The First Typo in a New Notebook” and “Summer at Sixteen”—have recorded Grammar/Collision data at the “separation from Commerce Collision” level (see [[product-grammar]]). Under the principle of [[DEC-001-minimal-wiki-first]], `wiki/products/` will not be created until actual content has accumulated; it will be created after step 5 of the v0.4 plan (developing 8–10 representative products to Product Page level) has actually been carried out.

# Product Grammar

## Meaning in OTHER GOODS

Product Grammar is Layer 1 of product brainstorming. It asks:

> What kind of “thing that cannot be a product” is this in the first place?

Product ideas do not begin with a Commerce function. They begin with properties found in reality—experiences, states, time, relationships, possibilities, and boundaries—and only then enter a commerce system.

Commerce functions such as Return, Review, Cart, and Shipping are not Product Grammar. They belong to Layer 2, Commerce Collision: the surface on which a product's identity collides with the shopping system. See [[DEC-007-ontology-before-commerce]] for the principle of separating the two layers. See [[commerce-as-medium]] for the context in which Commerce acts as an amplifier, classifier, and collision device rather than as the origin of an idea.

## Status Warning

This taxonomy is not confirmed. Most names and definitions of the 14 Grammars below are `llm-proposed` and have not been individually approved by the user. Only the two-layer structure separating Product Ontology from Commerce Collision can be described as `jointly-developed`. The final number of categories and their boundaries remain open in [[Q-003-product-grammar-taxonomy-scope]].

**(2026-09-04)** Two surveys of the 30-item v0.4 collection criticized this taxonomy more strongly. Both judged it over-segmented. Claude's sharpest criticism was that “the current 25 are closer to labels retrofitted to 30 names than discovered classifications.” This criticism is `llm-proposed` and does not resolve [[Q-003-product-grammar-taxonomy-scope]]; it makes the question more urgent.

## Grammars

### Irreversible

Something that cannot be restored once it has passed or become known.

- The Last Ten Minutes Watched Without Knowing the Ending
- Question 5 Before Seeing the Answer
- The First Typo in a New Notebook

### Non-standardizable

Something that can be classified under one name while its actual content differs for each person.

- Summer at Sixteen

### Location-bound

Something bound to a particular place or moment that loses its identity when moved.

- The Reverse Side of a Building Visible Only from a Bus Window
- The Weather Where I Am Not Right Now
- The Alley You Pass Only After Taking a Wrong Turn
- The Far Left of the Stage, Invisible from My Seat

### Shared-but-different

An event shared by several people whose experiences are not identical.

- Two People Caught in the Same Rain
- Two Hours Spent Watching the Same Film
- Different Evenings Spent at One Table
- That Day for Five People in One Photograph
- Different Years Spent in the Same House

### Boundaryless

An experience or state whose precise beginning and end are difficult to determine.

- The Day Summer Began
- The Room That Became Familiar at Some Point
- The Moment a Friend Became a Friend
- The Road You Stopped Taking at Some Point
- The Last Time You Did Not Know Was the Last

Because the poetic sentence itself can become the work, this Grammar needs balance with Commerce.

### Unrecorded

Something that clearly existed but was not recorded.

- One Day of a Trip Without Photographs
- Six Minutes of Conversation No One Recorded
- What Happened Outside the Camera Frame
- A Purchase Without a Receipt
- Something Unrecorded from the Beginning, Not Deleted

Core distinction: existed ≠ recorded.

### Repeatable-but-not-identical

Something repeatable that is never completely the same.

- A Different Commute on the Same Route Every Day
- The Slightly Different Three Minutes Before Falling Asleep
- The Thirtieth Rain Seen Through the Same Window
- The First Ten Minutes of Every Monday
- The Hundredth Greeting to the Same Person

This Grammar is an important counter-axis to “something special because it was the first.”

### Unassignable

Something whose owner is difficult to determine in the first place.

- Silence Between Two People
- Another Person's Laughter Heard on the Street
- Sunlight Through the Same Window
- Warmth Left on a Park Bench
- The Heat Felt by Everyone in One Room

### Counterfactual (Unlived Possibility)

Something that did not happen but could readily have happened.

- The Last Bus Not Taken
- The Next Conversation After an Unsent Reply
- The Taste of the Menu Item Not Chosen
- Ten Minutes in the Room Not Entered
- The Second Day of a Trip Not Taken
- The View You Would Have Seen on a Different Route

### Overabundant (Invisible)

Something too common to be perceived or valued in its original unit.

- The Third Door Opened Today
- Twelve Stairs Never Counted
- Twenty-One Seconds When No One Spoke in the Elevator
- The Forty-Seventh Rain This Year
- The Eighteenth Crosswalk Seen This Week

### Unitized (Scale-distorted)

Something so large or small that dividing it into product units is itself strange.

- Three Seconds of the Sea
- 0.01% of a City's Morning
- Twelve Drops from One Rainfall
- 0.4% of August

Further exploration is needed: a concrete rule must keep this from ending as poetic number play.

### Naming-dependent

Something whose nature or perception changes the moment it is named.

- The Room Before Calling It “Home”
- A Place Before Adding It to Favorites
- A Store Before Calling It Your Regular
- Yesterday Before Calling It a Memory
- A Choice Before Admitting It Was a Mistake

### Value-ambiguous

Something whose value can differ sharply—positively or negatively—between people.

- Two Hours from a Canceled Appointment
- An Afternoon Left Alone
- Twenty Minutes of a Meeting That Ended
- An Evening Without a Reply
- The Last Train Missed

### Identity-drifting

Something whose unchanged name no longer carries the same meaning over time.

- “These Days” from Five Years Ago
- Something That Used to Be New
- A Distance That Was Once Close
- The Latest Thing That Is No Longer Current
- Ten Minutes That Seemed Long at First
- ₩10,000 That Was Expensive Then

`Yesterday` can be treated as a representative existing candidate for this Grammar.

## Consolidation Proposals (Unadopted)

`llm-proposed`, unadopted. Two v0.4 surveys on 2026-09-04 independently proposed consolidating the taxonomy. Their shared proposals are to absorb Scale-distorted into Unitization and merge the Shared-but-different family with other Grammars.

Claude additionally proposed combining Non-standardizable, Shared-but-different, and Perspective into “One SKU / Many Referents”; absorbing Accumulation into Repeatable-but-not-identical; absorbing Viewpoint-dependent into Location-bound; absorbing Unlived Possibility into Counterfactual; and **demoting Irreversible from a top-level Grammar to a background property shared by other Grammars**.

The surveys diverge on Counterfactual itself. Gemini recommends dropping it, while Claude recommends keeping it but replacing regret-driven instances.

See [[v0.4-evaluation]] for details.

## New Grammar Proposals (Unadopted)

`llm-proposed`, unadopted. Together the two surveys proposed 11 new Grammars. All point in the same direction: away from memory, time, and first-person experience and toward material, perception, institutions, and industry.

One notable convergence is that Gemini's Perceptual Threshold and Claude's Below Threshold were proposed independently and describe nearly the same concept.

The individual list is not repeated here. See [[v0.4-evaluation]].

## Separation from Commerce Collision

Record Product Grammar and Commerce Mechanic as separate data.

**The First Typo in a New Notebook**

- Product Grammar: Irreversible / Condition-changing
- Primary Commerce Collision: New ↔ Used
- Secondary Commerce Collision: Return / Resale / Condition

**Summer at Sixteen**

- Product Grammar: Non-standardizable / Personal Experience
- Primary Commerce Collision: SKU ↔ Individual Experience
- Secondary Commerce Collision: Review / Rating / Ownership

A commerce function is not a product's identity. It is the “surface of collision” created when that product enters a shopping system.

## Open Problems

- Can one product belong to several Product Grammars at once?
- How many categories should the final Product Grammar taxonomy have?
- Are Physical Shipping, Packaging, Weight, and Damage Product Grammars or Commerce Mechanics?
- How can Grammars such as Boundaryless and Counterfactual be made concrete enough to avoid appearing excessively poetic?
- Beyond Negative Value, what Product Ontologies can extend the idea of “products one does not want”?
- How should the v0.4 surveys' criticism of over-segmentation be addressed—the claim that 25 classifications are retrofitted labels rather than discoveries?
- [[Q-005-commerce-generative-vs-display]] reopens the Layer 1 Product Ontology / Layer 2 Commerce Collision distinction itself. If Commerce is not merely a collision device but an ontology-producing institution, does the separation remain valid?

These questions are tracked in [[Q-003-product-grammar-taxonomy-scope]].

# Conceptual Contrast

## Definition

The central tension of OTHER GOODS comes from the collision between **a completely normal commercial system** and **things that system cannot treat as products**. When the discussion expanded toward blockchain and a time-based economy, the user judged that it had strayed too far and explicitly returned this conceptual contrast to the center.

## Operating Rule

> Reproduce the commercial system perfectly, while keeping the things it handles noncommercial.

Do not create strangeness through damaged UI, jokes, or explanatory copy. The store behaves consistently, as though it genuinely believes these things are ordinary products.

## Product Test

The following review framework was proposed by an LLM during the conversation and has `attribution: llm-proposed`.

When reviewing a candidate, ask:

- Is this normally bought and sold as a product?
- Does its meaning change when placed in a store?
- Would it be the same work in a medium other than a store? If so, the collision may be weak.
- Which commerce function—price, inventory, shipping, ownership, returns, reviews, or another—creates the strongest collision?

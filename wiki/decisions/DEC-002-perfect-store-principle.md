# DEC-002 — Perfect Store Principle

## Status

accepted (2026-09-03)

## Context

The initial idea was to place unsellable content inside a conventional e-commerce UI and create friction between those two systems. The user made this more concrete: technically and by the standards of a shopping service, anyone should judge the store to be “well made.”

## Decision

Build the store's technical, visual, and usability quality so that it stands beside real commercial services without deficiency. The work does not reveal itself through a low-quality store, broken UI, or joke behavior. Visitors first experience a well-made store, then ask what it sells.

## Why

If the store itself is strange, visitors immediately classify it as parody or an “art website.” The more normally the store operates, the stronger the friction created by the nonproducts it handles.

## Consequences

Apply the principle as follows (`attribution: llm-synthesis`):

- UI/UX, speed, search, product discovery, mobile experience, cart, checkout, and order flow are all evaluated as part of the work's quality.
- Deliberately strange UI, meme language, and explanatory copy conflict with the principle.
- Artistic intervention should arise from the consequences of complete operation, not from damaging the store's operation.

# DEC-007 — Put Product Ontology Before Commerce Collision

## Status

accepted (2026-09-04)

## Context

By R&D Collection v0.3, Commerce functions themselves—Return Grammar, Review Grammar, Cart Grammar, and Shipping Grammar—were being treated as Product Grammars. The user observed that “the entire Product Grammar is excessively e-commerce-like” and “focuses too much on products whose meaning grows when they meet e-commerce data.”

## Decision

Separate product analysis into two layers. Layer 1, Product Ontology or Product Grammar, asks: “What kind of ‘thing that cannot be a product’ is this in the first place?” Layer 2, Commerce Collision or Commerce Mechanic, asks: “Which commercial assumption breaks when it is treated as a product?” Ideas begin in Layer 1, while Commerce acts as amplifier, classifier, and collision device. The source wording is: “Do not begin with ‘let's make a product that collides with Returns.’ Begin with ‘what strange kinds of experiences and states exist in reality?’”

## Why

When the project narrows around Commerce functions, OTHER GOODS is reduced to “a meta-work that deconstructs an e-commerce system.” A good product need not be Commerce-native. Ontologically strange products and products that come alive only within Commerce should coexist, preventing visitors from classifying the work too quickly under one category. This purpose aligns with the operating rule already established by [[conceptual-contrast]].

## Alternatives Considered

The alternative of retaining the Commerce-native direction and extending v0.3 without change was rejected. There had already been a warning that too many Commerce-native products could narrow the work into a conceptual-art joke about e-commerce functions, and that risk appeared in v0.3.

## Consequences

Record Product Grammar and Commerce Mechanic as separate data. A Commerce function is not a product's identity, but the “surface of collision.” The previous Return, Review, Cart, and Shipping Grammar frame is rejected. What is confirmed is the two-layer *structure*; the 14-part taxonomy beneath it remains `working`. See [[Q-003-product-grammar-taxonomy-scope]].

## Challenge (2026-09-04, Unresolved)

Claude's v0.4 survey explicitly challenged this decision: “This hypothesis is partially wrong—the strongest products in v0.4 (05, 07, 09, and 10) are all generated by Commerce, and a literal application of the hypothesis would have prevented those four from being made.” Claude proposed an alternative formulation: “Commerce is not an amplifier but an ontology-producing institution.”

Gemini's survey moved in the opposite direction. It placed the same three products—05, 07, and 10—in the Bottom 8 and thereby supported this decision. The two surveys directly conflict.

**Because the surveys are `llm-proposed`, they do not invalidate this decision. DEC-007 remains valid with `confirmed` status.** The challenge remains open in [[Q-005-commerce-generative-vs-display]], and the decision will not change without the user's judgment.

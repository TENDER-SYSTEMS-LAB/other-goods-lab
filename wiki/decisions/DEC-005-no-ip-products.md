# DEC-005 — Do Not Use Products That Depend on Specific IP

## Status

accepted (2026-09-04)

## Context

Collection v0.1 included `The First Time Reading Episode 1 of Tower of God` as a candidate. The user judged that this product depended on a specific webtoon, IP, and generation, and could appear lightweight like fandom merchandise. This judgment is `user-originated`.

## Decision

Take the Collection in a non-IP direction. Remove `The First Time Reading Episode 1 of Tower of God` and replace it with **The Last Ten Minutes Watched Without Knowing the Ending**. The replacement's core idea is that content can be watched again, but the last ten minutes cannot be re-experienced without knowing the ending.

## Why

Specific IP creates generational and fandom dependence and makes the product feel lighter. This also conflicts with the user's direction that the work must not appear to be “a store that sells memories” or a “generational nostalgia shop.”

## Alternatives Considered

The conversation considered substituting another IP—candidates included `Eyes Before Watching Star Wars`—but rejected this because it repeated the same dependence on a generation and IP. The final decision was to avoid IP itself.

## Consequences

The replacement product, `The Last Ten Minutes Watched Without Knowing the Ending`, became a representative Irreversible Grammar case. IP dependence now operates as an exclusion criterion for future product candidates.
